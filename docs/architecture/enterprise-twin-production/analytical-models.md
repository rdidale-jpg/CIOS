# Analytical model catalogue

**Proposed recipes over existing objects and owners.** Owner identifies the existing decision/validation boundary. Runtime status describes the current bounded implementation, where one exists. A recipe creates no canonical framework object. Every output must retain subject, scope, period, input versions, assumptions, exclusions, counterevidence and material Unknowns. Published facts stay distinct from interpretation. Withdrawn or superseded inputs suppress or recompute dependent output through the existing owner.

Each model entry specifies **A** question, **B** required inputs, **C** optional inputs, **D** relationships, **E** sufficiency, **F** applicability/exclusion, **G** reasoning, **H** facts versus interpretation, **I** counterevidence, **J** output, **K** visual, **L** refresh, **M** owner, **N** runtime status.

## Descriptive

- **A** How does this enterprise create and capture value? **B** Segment, offer, customer, channel, capability and revenue/cost disclosures. **C** Retention, pricing and partner economics. **D** Offer → serves → customer, capability → delivers → offer.
- **E** Each material value proposition has an eligible business activity and customer basis. **F** Use group or segment independently. Exclude inferred allocation.
- **G** Map customer value, delivery and capture. **H** Separate reported economics from explanatory blocks. **I** Show cross-subsidy or missing channel evidence. **J** Scoped canvas with constraints. **K** Selectable Landscape blocks.
- **L** Recheck portfolio, segment and strategy changes. **M** Enterprise Model owner under ADR‑002. **N** Bounded segment descriptions implemented; full governed recipe proposed.

### Enterprise Value Chain

- **A** Which activities deliver an offer and where can value be lost? **B** Observed activity, trigger, output and role. **C** Costs, throughput and failure evidence. **D** Capability → supports → activity, activity → delivers → offer.
- **E** A traceable scoped service chain for the decision. **F** Conceptual chains can orient, with missing hand-offs labelled.
- **G** Separate observed stages from hypothesised hand-offs. **H** No inferred allocation from the chain. **I** Compare alternative channels and upstream causes. **J** Activity chain and open edges. **K** Selectable process lane.
- **L** Recheck upon channel, provider or control change. **M** Enterprise Model owner. **N** Conceptual chains implemented; reusable evidence-bound chain proposed.

### Technology and Supplier Ecosystem Model

- **E** Each material displayed edge has direct support and scoped endpoints. **F** Distinguish collaboration, contract, ownership and designation.
- **G** Map dependencies and alternatives. **H** Claimed exposure is analysis unless observed. **I** Include multi-sourcing, substitution and unknown workshare. **J** Dependency map with known limits. **K** Inside ecosystem.

### Enterprise Economics Model

- **A** What drives earnings, funding and cash over time? **B** Compatible original statements, segment note, dates, measures and definitions. **C** Cost drivers, investment and allocation disclosures. **D** Issuer/segment → reports → measures, activity → contributes only if established.

## External environment

### PESTLE

- **A** Which external forces could matter here? **B** Dated developments, jurisdiction, scope and enterprise exposure basis. **C** Peer/regulator evidence and secondary scenarios. **D** Industry force → potentially transmits through → operating activity.
- **E** Verified event plus evidenced applicability for a company-specific conclusion. **F** Context remains industry-scoped if exposure is missing.
- **G** Describe mechanism and tested applicability. **H** Event versus interpretation. **I** Show contrary indicators, timing and voluntary vs mandatory distinctions. **J** Force watch with bounded implications. **K** Forces cards.
- **L** Recheck on data release, policy revision and effective date. **M** Industry and Enterprise owners. **N** Selected force stories implemented; reusable recipe proposed.

### Porter's Five Forces

- **A** What constrains value capture in the defined market? **B** Boundaries, rivals, substitutes, entry barriers and buyer/supplier dependence. **C** Concentration, switching evidence and margin proxy. **D** Participant → competes/supplies/buys → scoped market.
- **E** Each assessed force has independently scoped evidence. **F** Do not score unobservable power or infer market-wide leverage from one account.
- **G** Compare leverage mechanisms qualitatively. **H** Statements of scale versus inferred bargaining power. **I** Unobserved contracts, policy changes and credible alternatives. **J** Five distinct force findings or Unknowns. **K** Market lens.
- **L** Refresh on structural or switching changes. **M** Industry owner and Enterprise reviewer. **N** General recipe proposed.

### Market and Competitor Mapping

- **A** Who is a genuine competitor for which customer and geography? **B** Named entity, market, offer, overlap and commercial status. **C** Adoption, independently measured shares and announced entries. **D** Enterprise → offers → product, product → competes in → market.
- **E** Same relevant scope and credible product overlap. **F** Exclude unsupported ranking and identity equivalence.
- **G** Group by evidenced overlap and show boundary cases. **H** Reported market facts versus inferred proximity. **I** Flag scope ambiguities and substitute alternatives. **J** Competitor map with comparability metadata. **K** Landscape/Forces selector.
- **L** Refresh on market, offer or entity change. **M** Industry and participant owners. **N** Existing participants and bounded contextual comparisons; model proposed.

### Industry Benchmarking

- **A** What can be compared fairly and why does it matter? **B** Values, entity, period, denominator, definition, currency and perimeter. **C** Adjustments with accepted method and exclusions. **D** Peer → reports → comparable measure.
- **E** Match every axis needed for the asserted comparison. **F** Same-period volumes can show scale only, without a ranking where denominators differ.
- **G** Validate comparability before calculating or displaying differences. **H** Reported values vs transparent calculations. **I** Flag missing denominator and state effect on interpretation. **J** Comparable rows plus blocked comparison reasons. **K** Explicitly labelled peer cards/bars.
- **L** Recheck new releases, restatements and weighting. **M** Industry and Financial owners. **N** Bounded FCA activity and typed financial comparisons implemented; broad benchmarking proposed.

## Strategic analysis

### SWOT

- **A** Which internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats change a decision? **B** Supported capability and constraint, market event and scoped exposure. **C** Segment and outcome evidence. **D** Finding → affects → business activity, force → transmits through → exposure.
- **E** A material claim in each asserted theme with evidence or explicit inference. **F** External strategic opportunity is separate from Pipeline.
- **G** Distinguish internal capacity, external change and their implications. **H** Findings are interpretation linked to facts. **I** Record alternate explanations and exceptions. **J** Supported themes with consequences and Unknowns. **K** Selectable quadrants.
- **L** Refresh when a linked fact or scope changes. **M** Enterprise owner. **N** Lloyds bounded views implemented; reusable qualification proposed.

### TOWS

- **A** Which strategic responses pair internal and external conditions? **B** Eligible SWOT themes, affected decisions and constraints. **C** Feasibility, resource and switching evidence. **D** Option → addresses → supported theme pair.
- **E** Each pair has a meaningful shared scope and plausible mechanism. **F** Omit pairs whose theme is unresolved at material consequence.
- **G** Compare strength/opportunity, weakness/opportunity, strength/threat and weakness/threat only where supported. **H** All options are alternatives. **I** Show opposing constraints and disconfirmation. **J** Paired options with trade-offs. **K** Linked option cards.
- **L** Re-evaluate when a theme invalidates. **M** Enterprise owner with ADR‑005 lineage. **N** Proposed.

### VRIO

- **A** Which resources plausibly sustain advantage? **B** Resource, value mechanism, competitor comparison, imitation barrier and organisational ability. **C** Patents, economics, adoption and alternatives. **D** Resource → enables → capability → supports → offer.
- **E** Evidence for each asserted dimension. **F** Do not infer rarity or defensibility from a brand or contract alone.
- **G** Evaluate value, rarity, imitability and organisational support individually. **H** Competitive endurance is interpretation. **I** Record substitutes and incomplete market coverage. **J** Resource profile with unresolved dimensions. **K** Capability evidence matrix.
- **L** Refresh on entry, substitution or capability change. **M** Enterprise and Industry owners. **N** Proposed.

### Risk and Material Pressure Analysis

- **A** Which evidenced exposures could constrain action and when? **B** Exposure, condition, consequence, affected service, horizon, mitigations and qualification basis. **C** Incidents, independent controls and threshold evidence. **D** Pressure → affects → scoped enterprise/service.
- **E** Direct applicability and materiality for qualified pressure, plus supported urgency for a time-sensitive label. **F** Hypotheses stay scenarios. No invented probabilities, risk score or burning platform.
- **G** Trace condition, potential consequence, response and counterevidence. **H** Disclosed risk, inferred exposure and accepted pressure stay separate. **I** Record mitigation and readiness uncertainty. **J** Bounded risk/pressure findings. **K** Forces with timing lanes.
- **L** Revisit when deadlines, exposure or qualifications change. **M** Existing Pressure owner under ADR‑026 and Enterprise owner. **N** Bounded pressure/risks implemented; broader recipe proposed.

### Scenario Planning

- **A** Which decisions hold under plausible alternate states? **B** Observable starting state, assumptions, horizon and causal mechanism. **C** Ranges with provenance and leading indicators. **D** Scenario → could affect → scoped capability/metric.
- **E** Explicit assumption boundaries and at least one rival scenario. **F** Exclude unsupported probabilities and forecast dates.
- **G** Trace condition, response, outcome and decision implication. **H** Separate observed baseline from possible futures. **I** Include contradicting observations and falsifiers. **J** Conditional alternatives and watch points. **K** Side-by-side scenario lanes.
- **L** Revisit trigger indicators and invalidated inputs. **M** Enterprise analysis owner. **N** Lloyds AI illustration is bounded; reusable recipe proposed.

### Strategic Options Analysis

- **A** Which responses remain defensible under the constraints? **B** Decision, verified drivers, alternatives, execution constraints and success measures. **C** Opportunity costs and independent outcome evidence. **D** Option → addresses → risk/goal, capability → enables → option.
- **E** Compare at least two feasible alternatives or state why only one is evidenced. **F** Reasoning does not establish a management decision or seller opportunity.
- **G** Compare scope, economics, reversibility and trade-offs. **H** Separate options from plans and qualified decisions. **I** Show adverse cases and constraints that could reverse preference. **J** Options with conditions and next questions. **K** Investigate comparison cards.
- **L** Refresh on decision, funding or evidence change. **M** Enterprise owner under ADR‑005. **N** Lloyds conditional options implemented; reusable recipe proposed.

## Change and disruption

- **E** Verify the stage and applicability for each item. **F** No implied timeline or rank when immature.
- **G** Group by observed/announced/scenario status and possible impact. **H** Release event vs analytical implication. **I** Include counterexamples and constraints. **J** Watch list with triggers and open issues. **K** Forces radar with categorical lanes.
- **L** Review on release, adoption or changed exposure. **M** Industry and Enterprise owners. **N** Proposed.

### AI-native Entrant Analysis

- **E** Verified entrant/offer and overlap for a named competitive finding. **F** Hypothetical assistants stay scenarios. Exclude undated launch/market-share speculation.
- **G** Compare product job, economics and operating constraints. **H** Separate issuer capability, entrant fact and scenario. **I** Consider distribution, protection, consent and access blockers. **J** Entrant evidence or bounded scenario. **K** Verified vs hypothetical lanes.
- **L** Refresh on launch, product uptake or policy change. **M** Industry/participant and Enterprise owners. **N** Scenario distinction implemented; named entrant recipe proposed.

### Enterprise Mechanism and Dependency Analysis

- **A** Which evidenced links explain a change and where is the chain weak? **B** Scoped typed endpoints, observed state changes, time ordering, and competing mechanisms. **C** Outcome evidence, process detail and comparable cases. **D** Programme → changes → capability → affects → outcome, with edge-level support.
- **E** Evidence for the specific asserted edges; causal language requires supporting time/alternatives evidence. **F** A map of related topics is a navigation aid.
- **G** Trace supported edges, flag hypotheses and test alternative mechanisms. **H** Facts, explanatory associations and causal conclusions stay distinct. **I** Show conflicting timing, confounders and missing links. **J** Inspectable chain with qualified gaps. **K** Core-renewal issue map.
