## Economic-system model

1. **Funding → earning assets.** Retail and Commercial deposits and wholesale debt fund a wider balance sheet than customer lending. Product mix, deposit pricing and wholesale maturities affect funding cost. Transfer pricing distributes net interest internally, so a division margin cannot be read as a legal-entity external spread. A structural hedge reduces sensitivity for stable balances. The repricing schedules of loans, deposits and hedges can move at different speeds. [S1 FY25 Note 4, pp. 228–229; S3 Q3 pp. 2, 6, 9]
2. **Lending, services, insurance and leases → income.** Loans produce interest. Transactions, wealth and protection earn other income on different bases. Rental income and asset depreciation sit on opposite sides of the leasing economics. Group underlying other income is broader than statutory net fees. Insurance administration assets are also distinct from bank deposits or Group earning assets. [S1 Note 4; S2 H1 pp. 21, 23–29, 64; S3 pp. 6–7, 9]
3. **Operating costs, remediation and impairments → earnings.** Operating costs include labour, estate, technology and other expenses. Remediation is separate on the underlying view. Expected credit loss charges depend on model scenarios, existing balances, portfolio mix and releases as well as deterioration. Statutory adjustments and tax then change profit available to shareholders. [S2 pp. 13–16; S3 pp. 2, 5, 7–8]
4. **Risk, liquidity and capital → investment and distributions.** Lending growth generally uses risk-weighted capacity, while risk transfer or securitisation may change it. Eligible liquid assets support near-term outflows. Capital generation can finance growth, acquisitions, reinvestment or distributions, conditional on regulatory and management buffers. Regulatory capital headroom is not a cash reserve or a technology budget. [S3 pp. 9–11; S4 KM1 pp. 3–4]

## Group economics

**Reported, consolidated Group.** S3 pp. 2 and 5 report 9M 2026 underlying net interest income of £11,084m against £10,106m in 9M 2025. Banking NIM moved from 3.04% to 3.21% (average interest-earning banking assets £460.4bn to £477.5bn). The bank-only NIM numerator is £11,470m, after removing £386m non-banking net interest expense. Statutory NII is £10,827m for the same nine months, a separate IFRS basis. At 30 September 2026 underlying customer loans were £495.5bn and deposits £502.8bn. September Group deposits can grow while Retail deposits shrink. The issuer describes year-to-date Commercial growth of £9.6bn and Retail reduction of £3.7bn. The £6.3bn Group difference is a balance change and does not establish organic flows, mix or rate sensitivity. Wholesale funding £109.9bn uses the revised debt securities and subordinated liabilities definition. [S3 pp. 2, 5–6, 9, 15]

**Reported fees, separate horizon.** Group statutory net fees were £1,002m at H1 2026 against £856m at H1 2025, after fees paid. Current account and card fees are principally Retail. Treasury and factoring fees arise in Commercial, and unit trust and insurance broking principally in Insurance, Pensions and Investments. The H1 Group £3,310m of underlying other income also encompasses leasing, insurance and investment items and cannot be added to statutory fee income. FY2025 statutory PBT £6,661m is a completed full-year observation, separate from the £6,625m for the first nine months of 2026. [S1 pp. 51, 229; S2 pp. 21, 64; S3 p. 5]

**Calculated attribution.** Reused Wave 3 bridges reconcile underlying net income £14,819m and statutory PBT £6,625m. Matching nine months, the £1,947m statutory PBT rise comprises £1,262m higher net income, £851m lower remediation, £70m higher operating costs, £312m higher underlying impairment, £35m higher restructuring and £251m better volatility/other. Lower remediation is a large comparator effect and cannot be treated as recurring productivity. The total cost/income ratio fell from 59.7% to 49.3%; on the issuer's basis excluding remediation it fell from 52.9% to 48.9%. Underlying impairment reached £930m versus £618m. Management attributed the increase partly to macro scenario recalibration, normalisation and portfolio growth, while reporting stable arrears. Thus higher impairment is evidence of a larger accounting charge rather than proof of a deterioration across all cohorts. [S3 pp. 2, 7–8]

**Capital and funding.** At the September 2026 reference date CET1 was 13.3%, against 14.0% recorded and 13.2% pro forma at December 2025. The apparent direction reverses with the comparator, reflecting the buyback adjustment. Reported nine-month capital generation was 153bp before the waterfall's dividends, buyback and acquisition. Risk-weighted assets moved from £235.5bn to £247.4bn. Pillar 3 gives LCR 143% on a rolling 12-month basis and NSFR 123% on a four-quarter basis. These are regulatory averages, not spot cash. Management's initial Basel 3.1 RWA reduction range is contingent on model approvals and is future guidance. [S3 pp. 2, 10–11; S4 KM1 pp. 3–4]

## Strategy, operating change and commercial investigation

**Reported strategy and testable interpretation.** Q3 nine-month banking NIM and net interest income improved, consistent with management's view that structural-hedge reinvestment supports income. However, mortgage refinancing and deposit competition were counterforces. Nine-month hedge income £5.2bn versus £4.0bn is a disclosed contribution, not an incremental amount to add to NII or a stand-alone forecast. Current-account retention and commercial deposit growth may matter more than aggregate volumes. [S3 pp. 6, 9]

**Targets and outcome.** Nine-month 49.3% cost/income and 17.9% return on tangible equity sit favourably alongside FY26 guidance of <50% and >16%, respectively, but do not settle the full year. Nine-month asset quality 25bp is consistent with c.25bp FY guidance on its stated basis. On unchanged basis, Q4 would need more than £3,816m underlying NII to clear the >£14.9bn annual threshold and less than £2,654m operating costs to stay below £9.9bn. Q3 actuals £3,806m and £2,370m are comparators, not forecasts. Nine-month generation 153bp also leaves more than 47bp to reach >200bp on the disclosed basis. The 2030 <45% efficiency target and c.£2bn gross savings are forward management ambitions separate from realised operating costs and net benefits. [S2 pp. 10–11; S3 pp. 1–2, 7–8, 10]

**Technology and change.** FY2025 Note 23 records £1,252m of additions and acquisitions in capitalised software enhancements, £1,270m amortisation and a £4,517m carrying amount at December. These are distinct accounting measures, rather than a total technology expense, cash spend or AI measure. [S1 Note 23 p. 280] The H1 CEO statement describes an approximately 35% reduction in Group run-and-change technology costs and modernisation of the estate as progress on the 2022–26 strategy. It does not supply the matched absolute spend base, a supplier budget or the net cost of any specific intervention. In the same statement, GenAI benefit in 2026 is an expectation, not independently verified cash or recurring savings. Envoy already exists, with controls disclosed under Group COO Ron van Kemenade and collaboration at Google Cloud brand level. Curve was completed in May and consumed a separately disclosed 7bp of capital; it is not an estimate of technology spend. [S2 pp. 7–11; S3 pp. 1, 8, 10–11; Wave 3 `LBG-4`]

## Landscape integration recommendation

Keep the LVIR-2 selection and Facts-first controls. If the existing Source and Financial owners separately authorise exact new use, begin with **Lending & income**. It currently uses Group PBT because lending evidence is unavailable. Show nine-month underlying NII, banking NIM and average earning assets together as different measures on the same period. Put statutory NII and the bank/non-bank perimeter reconciliation behind the basis drill-through. Insight can then show hedge reinvestment versus mortgage/refinancing and deposit-price effects, keeping magnitudes unallocated where unreported. Next, show loan and deposit stocks side by side with the dates in Funding. In Profit & costs, reuse the two existing candidate bridges, preserving lease depreciation, remediation and credit losses as separate drivers. Keep H1 divisions in an explicitly dated detail lens. Source withdrawal must suppress dependent displays and interpretation. No runtime change is part of this PR.
