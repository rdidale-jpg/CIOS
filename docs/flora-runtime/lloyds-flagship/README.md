## Commercial and architectural critique

A seller can quickly distinguish an existing AI capability from a legacy transition, follow the economic ambition to the operations at risk, and ask how a named service’s cutover and benefits are measured. The evidence establishes direction and constraints. It leaves the actual cost of parallel running, programme accountability, contract terms, customer outcomes, procurement and bank-specific risk magnitude unresolved. Three peer volumes cannot settle performance.

The draft adds a presentation composition under the existing Lloyds owner and one reviewed working policy candidate. A richer system-wide strategic map would need qualified Evidence and Observation records with accepted enterprise-specific relationship ownership. Named platform inventory, management ownership, financial attribution, genuinely comparable Group metrics and named AI entrant status require separate governed evidence. The draft makes no durable acceptance decision and leaves BT, Arm and easyJet untouched.
