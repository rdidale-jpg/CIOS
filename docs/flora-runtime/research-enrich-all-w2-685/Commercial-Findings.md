## Twenty sector foundations: economic depth without false completeness

RIE-1 supplied scoped GVA/output context but thin industry economics. This wave adds the ONS annual **intermediate consumption / output** ratios for A–S, 2023 versus 2024, and derives the complementary GVA/output residual. This is a useful common basis for discussing value creation. It is **not profit margin**: value added includes labour and other components; it is not free supplier budget. Ratios are rounded to 0.01 and describe 2024, not September 2026 costs.

For example, Energy supply's ratio moves from 0.71 to 0.76, while Agriculture moves from 0.63 to 0.60. Those differences motivate different economic questions; they do not identify a price shock's cause, an individual enterprise's exposure or money available for procurement. T is excluded by ONS because it has no intermediate consumption; it is not assigned a fabricated zero margin.

[Twenty-Sector-Foundations](Twenty-Sector-Foundations.json) contains every A–T boundary, prior-foundation locator/hash, new numerical or scope proposition, a distinct sector mechanism, counter-evidence, dimensional assessment and remaining demand. It separates broad sectors from narrow anchors: farming from forestry/fishing, England water from waste, hotel Group from all hospitality, English higher education from all education, and assessed care services from all UK health.

No foundation is marked complete. Competitive concentration, current UK supplier/customer contracts, operating outcomes and matched financial cohorts remain materially thinner than the common economic backbone. The largest continuing industry demands concern whole-sector comparisons beyond these anchors, not simply more source links.

## Banking: product mix and payment roles change the commercial reading

September BoE data distinguish new mortgage effective rates (4.63%) from outstanding-stock rates (4.03%). The **60 basis point cohort difference is not NIM**: funding costs, maturities and different loan populations are absent. Mortgage approvals are not drawdowns; the source explains why differently seasonally adjusted gross lending minus repayments does not equal its net lending series.

Household ISA and interest-bearing sight inflows coexist with withdrawals from other buckets. These are selected category movements, not a complete funding bridge or proof that a named bank lost customers. An ISA may itself be a bank deposit, so it cannot automatically be labelled disintermediation.

BoE operates RTGS and CHAPS. The PSR separately distinguishes scheme participation from processing charges in cards. Principal moved, operator service income, acquiring charges and interchange are different economic layers. **No Pay.UK tariff or fee pool is invented.** Those original/use barriers remain open.

The five-force analysis specifies the relevant cohort and evidence premise for each mechanism. Entry analysis is deliberately limited: settlement access is distinct from deposit-taking permission, and current PRA entrant/capital evidence remains a named demand. Technology supplier power is conditional on an actual bank service/licence relationship; a national CTP designation and a Group collaboration do not establish a legal-bank workload or open procurement.

The next commercially useful test is a matched product or service cohort's economic change **after existing management responses**, rather than treating a national rate headline as a bank pain point.

## Aviation: value is captured at different stages and on different clocks

Heathrow's original nine-month release provides a concrete airport revenue composition: £1,620m aeronautical + £592m retail + £472m other = **£2,684m**. Revenue less £1,243m adjusted operating costs reconciles to **£1,441m adjusted EBITDA**. These are source-defined airport results, not airline ticket revenue, route profit or UK Aviation GVA. Debt and regulatory assets have different legal/perimeter labels and are not combined here. Missing linked footnote definitions remain a later enterprise requirement.

Passenger traffic declined slightly while cargo tonnage increased on the same nine-month basis. This contradicts a single undifferentiated demand narrative. Airport passenger journeys are not unique people or easyJet's traffic. An airport seat factor is not an airline's load factor for an unrelated network.

Rolls-Royce's global Civil Aerospace disclosure distinguishes £3.4bn of Civil Aerospace LTSA flying-hour receipts from £86m net LTSA balance growth after RRSAs. Receipts, deferred service balances and revenue have different timing. This is an engine-service economics example, **not an easyJet supplier relationship**.

CAA charge-consultation requirements connect airport capacity/service and airline economics without supplying a particular carrier's tariff. Handling, airspace, leasing, distribution, package holidays, route yield and rotation utilisation remain separate mechanisms. NATS' official report announcement was located, but its financial tables were not acquired. Later easyJet financial/holidays tables remain index-only. The pack does not fill those gaps with Heathrow or RR figures.

## Technology: throughput, revenue and value captured are different

CMA reports UK public-cloud infrastructure customer spending of £10.5bn in 2024. Its provider share ranges use **UK+EEA** geography. Multiplying those ranges by UK spending would produce a misleading UK-provider revenue estimate. This is IaaS/PaaS infrastructure, not all SaaS, Technology GVA or global cloud-segment revenue.

Computacenter's issuer-hosted H1 release reports £8,927.5m gross invoiced income versus £6,845.2m revenue, with £657.9m gross profit. The £2,082.3m difference reflects recognition/perimeter, not a customer budget. Gross profit rose while gross margin declined; a high-volume mix shift is a material alternative to operating deterioration. These are global Group figures, not UK allocation. RNS distribution terms are an explicit unresolved use exception.

The eight role groups retain distinct economics: application subscriptions/licences; cloud capacity/platforms; data/AI consumption and implementation; cyber products/managed services; professional/managed delivery; channel principal/agency recognition; hardware and infrastructure; frontier R&D/commercialisation. They are **partly conceptual**, supported by bounded examples rather than eight fully measured markets. Membership overlaps are preserved; all 80 unbound profiles remain partial.

The commercial question is where an exact provider and business line captures value on a defensible UK/global basis, and which service dependency is actually evidenced. Directory presence supplies neither a customer contract nor buyer demand.
