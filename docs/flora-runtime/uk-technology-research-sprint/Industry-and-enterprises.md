# UK Technology economics and enterprise cohort

## Market perimeter and value chain

DSIT’s updated Digital and Technologies definition reports estimated **£408bn UK company turnover**, **£158bn UK GVA** and **1.33m company employees** in 2024. The publisher estimates £56,000 wages using the mean wage of the company containing the median employee, with greater uncertainty for wage and GVA estimates. Using these figures as a market size for IT spending would confound turnover with customer demand and intermediate resale. The government company population differs from CIOS’s operating layers, the SIC Digital Sector release and the broader digital-economy estimate. Do not sum layers or combine these denominators. [T01](Source-register.md)

| Layer | Economics and operational dependence | What to measure next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semiconductors and hardware | IP license and per-unit royalty differ from device/system sales. UK-relevant Arm design economics link to global fabrication and system assembly. | Royalty-bearing shipments and mix, exact design customer versus foundry roles, UK operations rather than global sales allocation. |
| Data centres and digital infrastructure | Site and connection rights → energisation → leased power and space → interconnection. Fixed investment, energy and time to utilisation drive economics. Geographic transport networks stay with Telecoms. | Energised vs requested MW, lease and connection milestones, interconnection mix, power and cooling costs, exact UK legal and JV scopes. |
| Hyperscale cloud | Compute, storage and managed services meter usage or take commitments. Capacity, depreciation, network and specialised silicon shape unit cost; licensing and portability affect switching. | UK workload economics and comparable unit costs; capacity installed versus committed. |
| AI and data infrastructure | Accelerators and cluster systems feed hosted training/inference and data services. Upfront hardware and power exposure differ from pay-per-use model and platform economics. | Funded versus announced capacity, usable GPUs, occupancy, cost per task and measured service outcomes. |
| Enterprise software and platforms | Seats, usage and renewals compound recurring income. Integration processes, data and user adoption can drive retention and create switching costs. | UK booked versus recognized sales, retention by cohort, channel and license portability. |
| IT services, integration and consulting | Project or time-and-materials fees pay for redesign and implementation. Utilisation, staffing mix, subcontracting and delivery risk drive margin. | Billable utilization, fixed-price mix, payroll and delivery productivity, actual completion/acceptance. |
| Managed services and outsourcing | Recurring service fees shift emphasis to transition costs, labour productivity and meeting service commitments. Existing delivery capability is a competing explanation for apparent demand. | Contract scope, service credits, renewals, retained responsibilities and delivery outcomes. |
| Cybersecurity and resilience | Subscription economics rely on retention, hosting cost and deployment scope. Contracted annual run rate, period revenue and cash are distinct measures. | UK recurring cohort, renewal budget, independent deployment outcomes and responsibility boundaries. |
| Distribution and channels | Vendor sourcing, fulfilment and advisory services earn product margin or net commission. The principal/agent judgment changes reported revenue without changing the underlying invoice. | Gross profit, working capital, accounting mix and actual call-off evidence rather than framework listings. |

## Comparable only within stated boundaries

- **Channel and integration.** Softcat plc Group FY26 gross invoiced income £4,719.7m equals £2,598.6m software + £1,405.2m hardware + £715.9m services. IFRS revenue £1,975.1m equals £278.5m + £1,399.4m + £297.2m. Gross profit £616.3m / gross invoiced income = 13.1% rounded. Underlying £215.1m operating profit less £18.7m non-underlying items gives £196.4m statutory operating profit. Group perimeter, fiscal year to 31 July 2026. Computacenter’s Wave 3 H1 Group measurement uses a different period and mix. Compare the recognition mechanisms, retaining each period and denominator. [T03](Source-register.md) [Wave 3 claims](../research-enrich-all-w3-689/Claims.json)
- **Cloud and software.** AWS Q3 segment sales and operating income of $46.0bn and $17.8bn imply 38.7%. Google Cloud $30.7bn and $11.1bn imply 36.2%. Both are global Q3 2026 segments with different service portfolios. Microsoft Cloud is a different aggregate and Azure growth alone supplies no UK revenue denominator. CMA’s 16 October update separates cloud actions from a proposed business software SMS designation. Check decision stage, actual remedy take-up and workloads instead of inferring switching. [Wave 3 claims](../research-enrich-all-w3-689/Claims.json) [T02](Source-register.md)
- **Capacity and investment.** Ofgem’s July 2026 release discussed 125 GW of Britain-wide demand applications and at least 80 GW associated with data centres in one passage, with about 73 GW referenced elsewhere in the same release. Different queue references need the underlying cut and denominator before ratios can be used. The proposed commitment fee and progress milestones were consultation items. North East Stargate capacity was announced in September 2025. The retained sources do not verify energisation or installed capacity at this cut. [T04–T05](Source-register.md)
- **Resilience.** Rubrik Q2 FY27 global subscription ARR $1.66bn at 31 July, quarterly revenue $427.3m and GAAP gross margin 78.4%. GAAP loss per share was $0.30. ARR is issuer-defined and assumes renewal under existing terms. Group growth and positive gross margin cannot establish UK profitability, net profitability or a buyer’s renewal budget.
