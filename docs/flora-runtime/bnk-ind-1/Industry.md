# How value and money flow through UK Banking

## Scope and system

Scope is UK-relevant deposit-taking, lending, transaction and servicing activities, plus the infrastructure, authorities and suppliers those activities use. A group’s consolidated worldwide results, finance and insurance GVA, a UK branch and a UK-authorised bank describe different populations. Include adjacent non-bank services only to explain an evidenced role. Framework draws on IT-001 Review and the existing Banking Foundation; Enterprise Memory retains ownership of enterprise state.

| Operating stage | Customer and activity | Where value, cash and risk move | Representative roles or evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demand and distribution | Households need payments, savings and mortgages; SMEs need working capital, accounts and collections; corporates need treasury, cash management, credit and cross-border services. Distribution includes branches, mobile, brokers and APIs. | Account onboarding, identification, sanctions screening and product selection incur costs. Customer acquisition and retention affect balances and fee flows. | BIE-2 industry and actor research; CASS supports current-account switching only. Do not extrapolate to corporate treasury or mortgage refinancing. |
| Funding and liquidity | Customers place deposits; the treasury function manages funding, liquidity and hedges. Wholesale liabilities can complement deposit funding. | Deposit balances are liabilities. Loan balances are assets. Equity absorbs losses, while liquidity and settlement assets meet cash outflows. Changes in rates pass through assets and liabilities at different speeds. | Nationwide Group retained accounts; November MPC policy decision. PRA supervision and FSCS protection are distinct. |
| Credit and servicing | Underwriting allocates credit for mortgages, SME and corporate facilities; servicing handles repayment, arrears and remediation. | Interest and fees accrue over time. Funding costs, impairment, servicing and capital intensity shape risk-adjusted returns. Higher policy rates can improve some spreads while weakening affordability and credit performance. | BIE-2 funding analysis; BIE-3 reconciliations. Compare by product, vintage, interest-rate terms and risk. |
| Initiate, authenticate and route | Customer, merchant, employer or PSP issues an instruction. Banks and non-bank PSPs provide interfaces; schemes set rules and technical standards constrain messaging. | Customer debit/credit amounts are principal. A payment charge, interchange, service fee, reimbursed loss and funds in transit are distinct accounting items. | Pay.UK rail pages; Open Banking role from BIE-2. Card acquirer, issuer, network and processor roles are distinct. |
| Clear and settle | Clearing reconciles instructions and obligations; settlement discharges obligations. Pay.UK lists Faster Payments, Bacs and Image Clearing separately; BoE operates CHAPS and RTGS in retained research. CHAPS is a high-value system and RTGS is settlement infrastructure. | Fast customer availability does not establish that every underlying obligation settled gross in real time. Liquidity use and timing differ by rail. | Fresh Pay.UK system and participation checks, retained BoE RTGS forum. Swift is a messaging cooperative/ecosystem role; a message, technical standard and settlement rail are different objects. |
| Support and outcomes | Fraud controls, complaints, dispute handling, accessibility and continuity sit across stages. Cloud capacity, core software, integration and outsourced operations can support multiple stages. | Fraud or disruption can create losses and remediation costs. Supplier usage, licences or services carry separate costs. Neither size nor legal designation quantifies bank dependence. | FCA numerical activity, Treasury designation and Lloyds disclosure. FOS dispute resolution and FCA conduct supervision carry different mandates. |

## Strategic groups and measurable mechanisms

| Business model | Value capture and cost drivers | Evidence / counter-reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diversified incumbent groups (Lloyds, NatWest, Barclays, HSBC contexts) | Deposit/loan spread and service fees, plus business mix. Distribution, compliance, change, technology and credit costs are separate. Monitor NII or margin on matched asset denominators, fee mix, impairment and CET1/RWA on consistent perimeters. | Group, ring-fenced legal bank and UK segment figures differ. BIE-3 NatWest’s £454.3bn total deposits and £453.4bn excluding central items are different measures. Its £110.9bn CAL stock change and £42.3bn acquisition-inclusive comparator growth are different comparisons. |
| Mutual mortgage and savings (Nationwide; wider mutual cohort) | Fund mortgages and other assets through deposits and treasury, with member value and retained surplus distinct from returns to shareholders. | Nationwide Group FY ended 31 March 2026, GBP millions: underlying income 6,378 less operating costs 4,021 and impairment 331 gives PBT 2,026. Add 1, deduct 138 and member rewards 399 to reach statutory PBT 1,490. The 127 integration cost sits within underlying operating costs. Virgin Money full year versus six months changes the comparator. Society-only FCA activity is separate. |
| App-led and specialist lenders (Monzo, Starling, OakNorth, others) | App-led customer acquisition, transaction activity and deposit funding versus specialist loan underwriting and sector risk. Starling's Engine role introduces software/licensing as a separate business mechanism. | BIE-2 issuer profiles describe differentiated mechanisms, with restricted candidate economics. Digital delivery does not guarantee scale, lower unit cost or a common fee mix. |
| Clearing and embedded banking (ClearBank) | Payment access/integration fees and deposit economics can coexist. Client integration, volume, platform operations and liquidity shape returns. | BIE-2 candidate FY2025 uses UK bank £12.2m PBT and Group £16.7m statutory loss, which cannot be joined as one earnings series. Separate normalised fees, actual statutory definitions and different growth disclosures before display. |
| Payment operator (Pay.UK Limited) | Runs common payment infrastructure and access arrangements. The analytical drivers are participant charging, payment activity, platform operations and resilience. Its corporate form is limited by guarantee. | Operator total receipts and individual rail charging schedules remain unverified. An aligned descriptive sample of two rails shows different profiles. Pay.UK reports 6.86bn Bacs payments (£6.05tn) and 5.55bn Faster Payments transactions (£4.84tn) for calendar 2025. Approximate amounts per instruction of £882 and £872 reflect rounded inputs and traffic mix. These figures measure principal, with no inference to operator fees or market share. Review-only. |
| Cloud, software and outsourcing | Consumption or contracted service fees can fund platform capacity, infrastructure, support, engineering and continuity. Core software licensing and managed operations can follow different pricing bases. | Google Cloud brand role is evidenced by Lloyds’ Envoy disclosure. UK legal-entity revenue, concentration, unit costs, subcontracting and exact workload remain unresolved. A model of cost drivers is analytical, with no assumed contract structure. |

**Comparative result:** Nationwide’s income-to-profit bridge explains member economics; a payment rail’s throughput shows infrastructure use; Google Cloud’s service model illustrates how a supplier creates value upstream. Compare those mechanisms explicitly. A pence-per-payment fee, bank return on equity, margin or bank-to-cloud spend comparison requires independently measured numerators, denominators, periods and entity boundaries. Use matching currency, units, annual/interim periods, legal geography, stock/flow, actual/forecast and statutory/underlying basis. Flag acquisitions and restatements before comparing. No evidence supports a quantified industry-wide profit-pool total in this tranche.

## National PESTLE and Banking applicability

| Lens | UK context → Banking mechanism | Counter-reading or scope boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Political | Payments policy and proposed access reforms can alter participation and governance. BIE-2 PSR-ACCESS is a consultation input. | Consultation and implementation dates differ; verify legal effect before changing regulator ownership. |
| Economic | The MPC increased Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4% at the meeting ending 4 November, published 5 November 2026. Repricing and credit affordability transmit through different balance sheets. | The May Nationwide results precede this decision. No demonstrated institution-specific earnings effect follows. |
| Social | Switching, digital access, branch access and service outcomes shape customer value and retention. Pay.UK reports 411,384 completed switches Jul–Sep 2026; named-provider net flows in that release cover Apr–Jun. Across matched July–September service totals, 2025 had 265,083 switches and 2026 had 411,384, a rise of about 55%. The sample is one scheme across two matching seasonal quarters, without a stock-of-accounts denominator. | Switching is a scoped current-account measure with no direct churn or overall bargaining-power estimate. Complaint activity is a different measure. |
| Technological | AI, APIs, cloud, core processing and payments rails change interfaces, controls and dependencies. | A disclosed rollout does not prove adoption, control effectiveness or an advantage across all banks. |
| Legal | PRA prudential requirements, FCA conduct, payments regulation and CTP oversight apply to different actors and actions. | Group affiliation, legal designation and actual scope of an important business service are separate. |
| Environmental | Energy costs, financed exposures and client mandates can affect operating costs and credit or supplier selection. BIE-2 National Trust material illustrates a customer governance preference. | No evidence of an award, breach, portfolio loss or sector-wide change from that source. |

## Porter Five Forces

| Force | Mechanism and evidence | Counter-evidence / test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rivalry | Incumbents, mutuals and challengers overlap in savings, transaction accounts and lending, but compete with different economics. Nationwide’s member value and CASS flow are distinct forms of competition. | Define product market, share denominator and geography before estimating concentration. Bank cases alone do not measure rivalry. |
| Entrants | Regulated challengers and non-bank access to specific rails can enter at different layers. BIE-2 REV-UK records a reported regulatory entry stage; Pay.UK lists mixed provider roles. | Do not match the rail's Revolut Limited label to Revolut Bank UK Ltd. Deposit permissions, access prerequisites, funding and customer acquisition remain barriers. |
| Buyers | Switching infrastructure can reduce account migration friction for eligible consumers and businesses. | Observed switching is selected behaviour. Measure inertia, price response and eligibility by cohort. |
| Suppliers | Payment operators, core providers and cloud services may be difficult to replace where access, integration or data migration is material. CTP designation is policy evidence for systemic resilience. | A named collaborator or provider designation measures neither concentration nor bargaining power. Test substitutability and documented service reliance. |
| Substitutes | Account-to-account payments and wallet interfaces can substitute at checkout or interaction layers. BIE-2 Open Banking milestone shows participation in an alternative route. | A cumulative payment count does not measure displaced card revenue. Product-level substitution requires transaction and counterfactual evidence. |

## Dated change and relationship proof

**UK → Banking → Enterprise.** HM Treasury published the four CTP designations on 10 July 2026, effective 13 July. Banking relevance is continuity when a provider supplies important services. Lloyds Banking Group’s Envoy description names Google Cloud at brand scope and describes monitoring/controls. This gives a reason to investigate capability exposure and continuity. It does not identify a Lloyds Bank plc–Google Cloud EMEA Limited contract, critical workload, performance issue or buying intent. Keep the issuer HTML date (1 May) and conflicting linked variant (28 April) distinct; suppress exact-day display pending reconciliation.

**Positive cross-enterprise finding.** Pay.UK Limited is a company limited by guarantee (10872449). Its scheme participant publication expressly names Lloyds Bank plc and Nationwide Building Society under Faster Payments. The supported relationship is operator-listed scheme participation with distinct corporate and system endpoints. It does not prove bilateral contract terms, technical access subtype, volumes or continuity architecture. No positive Google legal contracting edge was verified.

**Separate contrast.** Nationwide’s reports describe a savings and lending business with member-value and acquisition effects. Its payment participation is an operating role separate from Group earnings. The fresh Pay.UK pages add calendar-2025 throughput to the retained rail structure. The November MPC and CASS facts are dated review candidates. July CTP history and an announcement with competing publication dates are not a latest-news edition. The 17 BIE-2 candidates retain their original status.
