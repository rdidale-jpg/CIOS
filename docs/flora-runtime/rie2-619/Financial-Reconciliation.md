Retail underlying other income rose **£130m**, while operating-lease depreciation rose **£129m**. That bridge leaves only£1m incremental other income after depreciation at this aggregate level. It is **not** an isolated leasing margin: other income contains additional activities. Management attributes lease depreciation to fleet/value effects and a Q2 used-car-price charge, partly offset by existing risk mitigation. It attributes lower Retail underlying operating costs to savings/lower severance, partly offset by growth/inflation. The next useful question is a product/asset/service-return decision within current remedies, rather than an unsupported demand for another generic cost-cutting platform.

Retail impairment increased£223m; Commercial's charge fell£49m. Management cites model changes, normalisation, growth and a favourable prior-year house-price comparator, while describing stable credit. An expense increase is observed; deteriorating customer cohorts, bad underwriting or failed systems are not established. [Four revised questions](Leadership-Agenda.md).

## Statutory versus underlying is explicitly bridged

H1 2026 statutory income **£10,626m**, expenses **£5,717m**, impairment **£616m**, PBT **£4,293m**. Underlying net income **£9,747m**, total costs **£4,915m**, impairment **£617m**, profit **£4,215m**. Note3 separately removes restructuring, volatility/other and insurance gross-up; statutory operating expenses include lease depreciation whereas underlying puts it in net income.

The **£1m impairment difference is an explicit insurance gross-up**, not unexplained rounding. Under IFRS17, relevant insurance fulfilment expenses sit in statutory insurance-service income and are grossed back into underlying costs. Both the row bridges and PBT bridges pass arithmetic checks.
