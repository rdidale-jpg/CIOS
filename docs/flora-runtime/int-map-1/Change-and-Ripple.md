# Change intelligence, ripple-through and the “What's changing?” product

## The change ladder (do not collapse it)

```text
Public source / news article (a publication, not an impact)
    ↓ verified or bounded attributed factual statement
Dated event or measurement (what happened / was announced / becomes effective)
    ↓ compare with prior state and identify the actual delta
Enterprise or UK/industry change (subject, period, mechanism, status)
    ↔ scoped relationship and evidence for affected entities/capabilities
Potential impact / industry pattern (derived assessment, not inherited fact)
    ↓ separate material-pressure qualification or commercial validation
Commercial hypothesis → qualified opportunity only via existing approved workflow
```

One news article may concern multiple events. Multiple articles may concern one event. An announcement is not completed rollout, proposed policy is not enacted, and publication date is not the event/effective date. Use BIE-2's existing event/publication/retrieval/effective-date distinction and deduplication. Avoid a ticker that merely restates articles.

## Two directions of ripple

**Downwards — UK → Banking → enterprise.** A UK instrument is associated with a PESTLE/legal or technology force. Existing industry operating functions make affected role classes *candidates for reassessment*. Only a verified enterprise relationship, legal entity/regulatory scope and effective timeframe justify asserting exposure. The system should mark uncertainty and show supported context; it must not copy the same mandatory duty into every bank's Model.

**Upwards — enterprise → Banking → UK.** A bank's published earnings/technology strategy changes its enterprise picture. Compare multiple independent, period/basis-compatible enterprises to form a qualified *candidate industry pattern*. One bank's plan is not a sector-wide trend. A UK-level interpretation requires additional economy/policy evidence or a genuine aggregatable industry model. Never overwrite the enterprise-specific fact as a generic industry truth.

**Sideways — enterprise ↔ providers/peers.** A verified supplier, customer, partner or regulated-provider relationship can identify peers worth reassessing. Relationship *kind* matters: regulator designation, vendor announcement, reported collaboration, product availability and executed contract are not equivalent.

## News as input, not a fourth ungoverned dataset

Suggested read-only progression using existing owners:
1. Ingest/select lawfully usable dated *events* through existing Source/Research ownership; retain original source links privately, original authored summary publicly.
2. Deduplicate event identity, distinguish publication, underlying event and prospective effective date, preserve issuer legal identity.
3. Derive a **delta**: new disclosed condition vs prior known condition; “announced” vs “implemented” vs “revised”. No numeric difference across non-comparable dates.
4. Associate to one or more *existing* PESTLE factors, Porter mechanisms, value-chain activities or Enterprise capabilities using exact supported relationship IDs or clearly labelled *reassessment candidate*.
5. Recompose views. Explicit time-window and change-type controls; invalidate via source/current-use/withdrawal. Do not auto qualify or write canonical data.
6. Permit later user-confirmed change-monitoring alerts only if separately commissioned and permissions are clear; no unapproved scheduler.

## UX options, including the requested third toggle

**Option 1 — a compact “Change pulse” within Landscape (least UI change).** Three noteworthy dated changes, each with a single sentence, date and industry/enterprise marker; select one to explore relevant existing views. **Advantages:** simple, low navigation change, preserves two modes. **Weakness:** risks cluttering the structural Landscape and has less room to connect emerging patterns. Recommended as *the smallest product proof* if only one or two real stories are eligible.

**Option 2 — a third “What's changing?” editorial toggle (recommended product destination after proof).** Keep `Landscape | Forces | What's changing?` as mutually exclusive editorial reading modes. The latter includes a **visual change timeline/ripple map**, 3–5 supported items with “what changed / where it matters / why now,” scoped time period, and links **within FLORA** to affected industry and enterprise views. Not a news feed, list of headlines or an evidence browser. Source proofs in Owner Research; legal notices unobtrusive. Use existing Top/Left/Main and scene navigation; do not create new visual asset inventory without design acceptance. At the UK level, a similar view aggregates national changes only, not a union of every enterprise news item.

**Recommendation:** design-preview Option 2, implement only after testing whether a third mode materially beats Option 1. No arbitrary frequency/importance scoring, no unsupported “critical” badges. Show meaningful dates. Three stories are enough to prove the integration pattern before wider bank expansion.

## Invalidation and example proof

If a source for Lloyds' Envoy controls is withdrawn or revised: suppress/change the associated enterprise AI story, its Industry “what's changing” appearance and any corresponding research candidate association. Do **not** automatically erase other bank research or legacy hypotheses. A source-pinned UI alone does not establish all ripple dependencies today; this is the minimal proposed contract to test.

If a new UK regulatory date supersedes an earlier announced date: preserve original and revision history, update the present effective-date editorial view, and re-evaluate target applicability. If the scope remains unknown, report “relevance under review” only to internal researchers; seller Main can simply state the dated policy change without naming a bank as affected.

## Product language boundary

User-facing pages should sound like an informed commercial analyst. Avoid `candidate`, `canonical`, `coverage`, `source admission`, `unsupported score`, source URLs, repeated legal caveats and extensive methodological clickdowns. Keep directly material scope, timing and uncertainty short. Preserve actual provenance, contradictions, precise claim types and rights in authorised research records.
