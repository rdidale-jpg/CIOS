## Enterprise Digital Transformation

`tech-strategic-digital-transformation` · Offer v1 · taxonomy v2 · developed · reviewed 2026-11-13

**Outcome:** Redesign an end-to-end business service and its operating model so an accountable enterprise sponsor can realise and sustain measurable customer and operational value.

**Target enterprise:** Large or multi-business enterprises with cross-functional customer journeys, fragmented process and technology ownership, an executive sponsor and an identifiable service outcome; not an isolated platform purchase.

**Segments:** Multi-business regulated enterprises; Multi-channel customer-service organisations; Enterprises coordinating several change programmes

**Overlapping categories:** consulting, engineering, software, data, managed

**Buyers:** CEO / COO transformation sponsor; CFO / benefits owner; Business service or customer-experience owner; CIO / enterprise architect; Change and workforce lead

### Economic value hypothesis

Less avoidable journey rework and failure demand; Improved service completion and customer experience; More effective allocation of change investment.

**Measures:** End-to-end service completion and time-to-serve; Repeat contacts and avoidable rework; Sustained adoption and benefits-owner acceptance

**Method:** Compare customer-approved baseline and future-state service outcomes; monetise only evidenced volume × attributable unit-cost change, less implementation, transition and recurring operating costs. Avoid double counting with other programmes.

**Costs:** Change delivery and integration; Workforce adoption and service transition; Ongoing platform and operating costs

**Disconfirm if:** No material service gap, no accountable benefit owner, or a simpler process-only change has a better evidenced value case

**Value status:** Unvalued until customer baseline, attribution, costs and approval are established; amount is null.

### Structural triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business outcomes span disconnected process, data and technology owners | Trace a customer journey across teams; compare hand-off, quality and value measures | The service may be deliberately federated and meeting its objectives | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |
| Technology investment is not connected to owned, sustained business benefits | Review approved investment cases, benefit ownership and post-release operating measures | The benefits may already be governed effectively by existing programmes | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |

### Event-driven triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer confirms a board-sponsored business or service transformation mandate | Dated mandate, named accountable sponsor, bounded outcomes and decision rights | A strategy announcement may be aspirational without an active programme | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |
| Customer confirms an acquisition, channel redesign or operating-model decision affecting a service | Dated service scope and approved decision with dependency owners | The change may be handled by an incumbent programme without external support | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |

Every pattern remains **not observed**; importance and timing require customer validation.

### Entry plays

#### Outcome-to-operating-model value map

**Buyer:** Business service owner and CFO benefits partner

**Point of view:** Start from a customer outcome, not the next technology platform.

**Conversation:** Which end-to-end service outcome is materially constrained, and who owns the benefits after launch?

**Proof:** Map one authorised journey, baseline its pain points and compare process-only, incumbent and technology-enabled options.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; End-to-end service completion and time-to-serve; Repeat contacts and avoidable rework; Sustained adoption and benefits-owner acceptance; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Would process simplification and clearer ownership achieve the outcome without an enterprise technology programme?

**Follow-on:** A customer-owned service blueprint, benefits map and gated transformation roadmap

**Stop:** No accountable service outcome or baseline; incumbent plan already meets the outcome

#### Programme value and adoption rehearsal

**Buyer:** COO transformation sponsor and workforce lead

**Point of view:** A delivered system creates no value unless the operating model and behaviours change.

**Conversation:** How will teams adopt the new service and demonstrate sustained value rather than technical go-live?

**Proof:** Rehearse a bounded future-state service with representative users, operational controls and benefits owners.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; End-to-end service completion and time-to-serve; Repeat contacts and avoidable rework; Sustained adoption and benefits-owner acceptance; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Does the expected value survive adoption effort, transition cost and the simpler alternative?

**Follow-on:** Separately sponsored implementation increments with adoption, assurance and benefits gates

**Stop:** No workforce participation, transition capacity or benefits accountability

### Delivery and commercial scope

Customer-owned discovery and value case → options/design → separately authorised implementation waves → operating adoption and benefits review

Gated enterprise transformation programme with a customer-owned benefits case; discovery, design, delivery and adoption scopes contracted separately

**Seller requirements:** Business service and operating-model design; Customer research and benefits measurement; Enterprise architecture and optional systems integration; Workforce adoption and programme governance

**Proof / rights:** Permissioned comparable delivery reference and measured outcome with limitations; Named enterprise programme, domain and transition/adoption capacity; Verified IP, data, partner and professional rights; declared subcontractor dependencies; Independent review of claims, scope, conflicts and commercial assumptions

**Differentiation to validate:** Would process simplification and clearer ownership achieve the outcome without an enterprise technology programme?; Can the seller substantiate relevant enterprise-scale delivery, rights, capacity and independent review for this scope?

**Constraints:** No transformation savings, customer growth or delivery capacity asserted; Customer sponsorship, data rights, workforce participation and transition approvals required; Systems Integration is one possible delivery specialism, not the customer outcome

**Excluded / disconfirming cases:** No material service gap, no accountable benefit owner, or a simpler process-only change has a better evidenced value case; No seller capability, account demand, access, funding or procurement approval established

### Conditional follow-on opportunities

- Integration delivery (`tech-engineering-02`): Approved dependency change and verified IP/interface access. Conditional hypothesis; not customer request or approved cross-sell.
- Customer-service platform adoption (`tech-software-02`): Platform change wins against process-only and incumbent options. Conditional hypothesis; not customer request or approved cross-sell.

## AI-first Business Transformation

`tech-strategic-ai-first-transformation` · Offer v1 · taxonomy v2 · developed · reviewed 2026-11-13

**Outcome:** Redesign a business process, roles and controls around a justified AI-enabled operating model, with measurable benefits, human accountability and sustained organisational adoption.

**Target enterprise:** Enterprises with a repeatable high-value process, accountable business and risk sponsors, lawful data access and capacity for operating-model change; distinct from a standalone model experiment or generic chatbot rollout.

**Segments:** Knowledge-intensive regulated services; High-volume assisted service operations; Enterprises with a governed data and AI strategy

**Overlapping categories:** data, consulting, engineering, software, managed

**Buyers:** COO / business process sponsor; Chief data or AI officer; CFO / benefits owner; CISO / privacy and model-risk owner; HR / workforce and adoption lead

### Economic value hypothesis

Improved quality or throughput of a bounded business process; Less avoidable handling and rework where demonstrated; More effective human review and decision support.

**Measures:** Task quality and exception/rework rates; Human review effort and service completion; Observed adoption, unit operating cost and evaluation drift

**Method:** Value only observed process improvements against a non-AI baseline; subtract data preparation, change, model/runtime, integration, review and ongoing control costs. Capacity released is not automatically a cash saving.

**Costs:** Data and integration readiness; Workforce redesign, training and adoption; Runtime, evaluation, human review and control operations

**Disconfirm if:** AI does not beat the simpler baseline, review effort offsets gains, or an acceptable accountable operating model cannot be established

**Value status:** Unvalued until customer baseline, attribution, costs and approval are established; amount is null.

### Structural triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A repeatable business process has a measurable decision or knowledge-work constraint | Owner-approved process baseline, exception categories and current human control points | Process simplification, search or rules may outperform AI | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |
| AI pilots are disconnected from operating ownership, adoption and accountable controls | Map pilots to process owners, evaluation methods, human review and operating costs | The organisation may intentionally be learning before scaling and have no transformation need | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |

### Event-driven triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer confirms an executive-sponsored AI operating-model programme | Dated business mandate, process scope, accountable sponsor and risk approval path | A public AI strategy does not establish a funded change programme | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |
| Customer authorises a scale, redesign or stop decision after a governed AI pilot | Dated evaluation results, adoption feedback and a documented decision gate | The pilot may show that a non-AI alternative is preferable | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |

Every pattern remains **not observed**; importance and timing require customer validation.

### Entry plays

#### Process and role redesign value case

**Buyer:** Business process sponsor, workforce and risk partners

**Point of view:** The opportunity is a better business process, not more AI usage.

**Conversation:** Which process outcome warrants changing work allocation, controls and accountability?

**Proof:** Map tasks and exceptions, compare process-only, rules and AI alternatives; specify human review, data rights and an owner-agreed evaluation.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Task quality and exception/rework rates; Human review effort and service completion; Observed adoption, unit operating cost and evaluation drift; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Would a rules-based or process-only alternative achieve the same outcome with lower total risk and cost?

**Follow-on:** A gated AI-enabled operating-model and benefits proposal

**Stop:** No lawful data access, accountable process owner, workforce participation or safe non-AI baseline

#### From pilot to governed operating model

**Buyer:** Chief AI officer and COO adoption sponsor

**Point of view:** A successful demonstration is not evidence of sustainable enterprise value.

**Conversation:** What must change in skills, oversight, service support and process ownership before scaling?

**Proof:** Review permitted pilot evidence and rehearse operating roles, fallback, evaluation drift checks and staged adoption.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Task quality and exception/rework rates; Human review effort and service completion; Observed adoption, unit operating cost and evaluation drift; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Do observed error rates, review effort or adoption resistance eliminate the case for scaling?

**Follow-on:** Separately authorised phased adoption, integration and evaluation service

**Stop:** Pilot does not beat the baseline, human accountability is absent, or ongoing operating costs are unowned

### Delivery and commercial scope

Customer-owned discovery and value case → options/design → separately authorised implementation waves → operating adoption and benefits review

Enterprise AI-enabled transformation programme with business, risk and adoption gates; runtime and platform spend require separate authorisation

**Seller requirements:** Business process and workforce redesign; Data protection and model-risk management; AI evaluation against non-AI alternatives; Enterprise integration and operational support; Benefits attribution and adoption design

**Proof / rights:** Permissioned comparable delivery reference and measured outcome with limitations; Named enterprise programme, domain and transition/adoption capacity; Verified IP, data, partner and professional rights; declared subcontractor dependencies; Independent review of claims, scope, conflicts and commercial assumptions

**Differentiation to validate:** Would a rules-based or process-only alternative achieve the same outcome with lower total risk and cost?; Can the seller substantiate relevant enterprise-scale delivery, rights, capacity and independent review for this scope?

**Constraints:** No autonomous decisions, paid model calls or external data transfers authorised by FLORA selection; AI, data, IP, workforce and production approvals remain separate; No guaranteed productivity uplift, headcount saving or model accuracy

**Excluded / disconfirming cases:** AI does not beat the simpler baseline, review effort offsets gains, or an acceptable accountable operating model cannot be established; No seller capability, account demand, access, funding or procurement approval established

## Enterprise Cybersecurity & Resilience

`tech-strategic-cybersecurity-resilience` · Offer v1 · taxonomy v2 · developed · reviewed 2026-11-13

**Outcome:** Improve an enterprise’s ability to protect and recover a critical business service through a prioritised, evidenced cyber control, resilience and assurance programme.

**Target enterprise:** Enterprises with critical-service dependencies, multi-provider controls and a CISO/business owner mandate; existing monitoring and identity controls must be assessed rather than presumed absent.

**Segments:** Critical-service and regulated enterprises; Multi-cloud or multi-supplier operating estates; Enterprises seeking independent control and recovery assurance

**Overlapping categories:** cyber, consulting, managed, cloud, network

**Buyers:** CISO / enterprise risk sponsor; Critical business-service owner; COO / resilience and continuity lead; Audit / assurance and legal partners; CIO / infrastructure and identity owners

### Economic value hypothesis

Lower exposure to consequential service disruption where controls are shown to improve; Less duplicated assurance and remediation effort; More defensible risk prioritisation and recovery decisions.

**Measures:** Tested recovery and escalation completion; Critical dependency/control ownership coverage; Evidence-backed remediation closure and assurance rework

**Method:** Use owner-approved risk and service-impact scenarios; quantify only supported exposure and control costs, with assumptions and ranges if later evidenced. Do not turn an unobserved breach probability or avoided fine into a promised saving.

**Costs:** Control and service engineering; Assurance and recovery exercises; Ongoing security operations and supplier coordination

**Disconfirm if:** Current controls demonstrably meet risk and recovery objectives, or proposed changes do not improve the customer-approved scenario

**Value status:** Unvalued until customer baseline, attribution, costs and approval are established; amount is null.

### Structural triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical-service control and recovery accountability spans several providers | Permitted service maps, control ownership, existing assurance and recovery obligations | Existing controls and assurance may already address the material risks | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |
| Security coverage is measured as tooling rather than tested business-service resilience | Review recovery exercises, dependency assumptions and owner-approved control evidence | An existing risk programme may already prioritise and test the right outcomes | Revalidate the operating baseline after service, ownership or solution changes; no numeric half-life |

### Event-driven triggers

| Pattern | Observable signal | Rival / disconfirming evidence | Revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer authorises a review after an incident, exercise or assurance finding | Dated, permitted finding with materiality, existing controls and an accountable remediation owner | A public incident or audit does not establish missing controls or supplier demand | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |
| Customer confirms a material service, supplier or operating-environment change | Dated change scope, affected critical services and agreed risk decision | The existing security team or incumbent may cover the change within its remit | Reconfirm dated catalyst, scope and current decision window; an announcement can expire or already be addressed |

Every pattern remains **not observed**; importance and timing require customer validation.

### Entry plays

#### Critical-service resilience challenge

**Buyer:** Business-service owner and CISO

**Point of view:** Protect the service outcome; do not start by assuming another security tool is needed.

**Conversation:** Which authorised scenario could impair this service despite the controls already in place?

**Proof:** Map one service’s controls and dependencies, then run a consented tabletop against recovery and escalation obligations.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Tested recovery and escalation completion; Critical dependency/control ownership coverage; Evidence-backed remediation closure and assurance rework; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Do the existing controls and recovery evidence already address the scenario adequately?

**Follow-on:** Prioritised, owner-approved resilience and control improvement programme

**Stop:** No material residual risk, no written exercise scope or no authorised access to existing control evidence

#### Control assurance and remediation roadmap

**Buyer:** Audit assurance partner and enterprise risk sponsor

**Point of view:** An assurance opinion must be supported by scoped evidence and independent challenge.

**Conversation:** Which control outcome lacks decision-grade evidence, rather than merely a new dashboard?

**Proof:** Review a redacted evidence sample; compare control design, operation and recovery observations without live exploitation.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Tested recovery and escalation completion; Critical dependency/control ownership coverage; Evidence-backed remediation closure and assurance rework; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Would better use or verification of incumbent controls remove the gap without a transformation programme?

**Follow-on:** Separately authorised control engineering, identity, monitoring or retest package

**Stop:** Conflicts impair independence, evidence access is denied, or testing rights cannot be established

### Delivery and commercial scope

Customer-owned discovery and value case → options/design → separately authorised implementation waves → operating adoption and benefits review

Scoped enterprise risk, resilience and assurance programme; independent assessment, remediation and recurring operations contracted with clear conflicts and rights

**Seller requirements:** Enterprise security and critical-service risk architecture; Operational resilience and recovery facilitation; Independent control assurance with declared conflicts; Multi-supplier remediation and identity/security engineering

**Proof / rights:** Permissioned comparable delivery reference and measured outcome with limitations; Named enterprise programme, domain and transition/adoption capacity; Verified IP, data, partner and professional rights; declared subcontractor dependencies; Independent review of claims, scope, conflicts and commercial assumptions

**Differentiation to validate:** Do the existing controls and recovery evidence already address the scenario adequately?; Can the seller substantiate relevant enterprise-scale delivery, rights, capacity and independent review for this scope?

**Constraints:** No intrusive testing, live access or incident handling without written authority; No certification, regulator acceptance, absent-control or zero-breach claim; Existing controls and incumbents are counter-evidence to a greenfield pitch

**Excluded / disconfirming cases:** Current controls demonstrably meet risk and recovery objectives, or proposed changes do not improve the customer-approved scenario; No seller capability, account demand, access, funding or procurement approval established

**Variants:** Cyber transformation: Prioritised control and operating-model change; Operational resilience: Critical-service dependencies, recovery and supplier hand-offs; Control assurance: Evidence-based independent challenge, conflicts and scoped retesting

## Complex Legacy Application Modernisation

`tech-strategic-complex-legacy-modernisation` · Offer v1 · taxonomy v2 · developed · reviewed 2026-11-13

**Outcome:** Modernise a complex application estate through a defensible retain, retire, rehost, replatform, refactor or replace strategy while preserving critical-service continuity and accountable value.

**Target enterprise:** Large enterprises with interdependent applications, legacy runtimes, multiple vendors, constrained change windows and significant transition risk; an isolated interface upgrade belongs in the existing narrow modernisation playbook.

**Segments:** Regulated multi-platform application estates; Enterprises with mainframe and distributed dependencies; Multi-vendor programmes coordinating retirement, migration and replacement

**Overlapping categories:** engineering, consulting, cloud, managed, software

**Buyers:** CIO / CTO programme sponsor; Business application and critical-service owners; Enterprise architect / application estate lead; CFO / portfolio benefits owner; Vendor management, security and transition leads

### Economic value hypothesis

Retire demonstrably unnecessary run and support cost; Improve change capacity and remove measured dependency friction; Reduce supported obsolescence and transition exposure.

**Measures:** Verified retired run/support costs and contract obligations; Service parity, reconciliation and recovery observations; Lead time and dependency/ownership closure

**Method:** Compare whole-life retain and change options including support extensions, licences, vendor/IP rights, dual run, migration, reconciliation, exit and support. Count retired cost only when obligations actually end; avoid blanket cloud or rewrite savings.

**Costs:** Assessment, engineering and data transition; Dual run, support extensions and contractual exit; Operational readiness, rollback and long-term maintenance

**Disconfirm if:** Retention or a smaller upgrade has better evidenced whole-life value, or continuity and rights cannot be established

**Value status:** Unvalued until customer baseline, attribution, costs and approval are established; amount is null.

## Social Value & ESG Transformation

`tech-strategic-social-value-esg` · Offer v1 · taxonomy v2 · developed · reviewed 2026-11-13

**Outcome:** Turn material social and environmental commitments into owned, measurable delivery and reporting practices with defensible boundaries, attribution and stakeholder accountability.

**Target enterprise:** Enterprises with material social/environmental outcomes or explicitly applicable customer procurement requirements, a responsible sponsor and lawful measurement access; not a generic ESG badge, offset claim or assumed bid-winning service.

**Segments:** Suppliers evaluating in-scope central government social-value commitments; Enterprises improving material environmental inventory and accountability; Organisations with supplier/community outcome delivery responsibilities

**Costs:** Outcome delivery and partner coordination; Data collection, consent, measurement and verification; Operating/reporting controls and any separately scoped technology

### Entry plays

#### Commitment-to-delivery social-value map

**Buyer:** Procurement/contract owner and social-value sponsor

**Point of view:** A credible commitment needs attributable additional delivery, not an attractive corporate pledge.

**Conversation:** Which contract-specific social outcome is material, deliverable and additional to the existing baseline?

**Proof:** Map permitted commitments to delivery owners, partners, consented evidence and an attribution/verification plan; check policy and jurisdiction first.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Verified additional social outcomes within a stated contract/boundary; Inventory coverage, factor provenance and reconciliation exceptions; Observed delivery cost, reporting effort and attributable absolute environmental change; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Does the procurement requirement actually apply, and are the proposed outcomes additional rather than relabelled existing activity?

**Follow-on:** Separately scoped social-value delivery and outcome-verification programme

**Stop:** No applicable requirement or stakeholder outcome; no delivery/partner rights; additionality cannot be evidenced

#### Environmental boundary and decision baseline

**Buyer:** Sustainability lead and CFO reporting partner

**Point of view:** An emissions inventory supports decisions but is not proof of decarbonisation or legal compliance.

**Conversation:** What organisational/activity boundary and decision require better environmental measurement?

**Proof:** Review a permitted sample of activity data, emissions factors, ownership, uncertainty and reconciliation; distinguish absolute change, intensity and boundary changes.

**Evidence:** Customer-permitted scope and baseline; Verified additional social outcomes within a stated contract/boundary; Inventory coverage, factor provenance and reconciliation exceptions; Observed delivery cost, reporting effort and attributable absolute environmental change; Permissioned seller delivery, rights and capacity evidence

**Disconfirming question:** Would a transparent correction of existing data and boundaries meet the need without new technology or a reduction claim?

**Follow-on:** Gated data ownership, measurement controls and separately authorised environmental improvement programme

**Stop:** No lawful data access, unsupported factors, double counting or an unreviewed green/reduction claim

### Delivery and commercial scope

Customer-owned discovery and value case → options/design → separately authorised implementation waves → operating adoption and benefits review

Scoped social/environmental outcome and operating-model transformation with separate delivery, measurement and assurance responsibilities; no contingent bid-win or guaranteed carbon/financial return

**Seller requirements:** Social-value outcome and stakeholder design; Environmental inventory and data-quality methods; Procurement/jurisdiction applicability review with qualified advisers; Supplier/community delivery governance and consent; Measurement, attribution and assurance coordination

**Proof / rights:** Permissioned comparable delivery reference and measured outcome with limitations; Named enterprise programme, domain and transition/adoption capacity; Verified IP, data, partner and professional rights; declared subcontractor dependencies; Independent review of claims, scope, conflicts and commercial assumptions

**Differentiation to validate:** Does the procurement requirement actually apply, and are the proposed outcomes additional rather than relabelled existing activity?; Can the seller substantiate relevant enterprise-scale delivery, rights, capacity and independent review for this scope?

**Constraints:** PPN 002 is not a universal UK, private-sector or devolved-procurement duty; verify the actual contracting authority, scope, documents and applicable policy; GHG inventory, ESG reporting, social value and actual emissions reduction are different claims; No legal opinion, accreditation, carbon neutrality, bid access or customer demand asserted; No personal/community data collection, offsets or environmental claims without separate rights and review

**Excluded / disconfirming cases:** Policy is out of scope, outcomes are not additional/material, existing reporting suffices, or attribution/measurement cannot withstand review; No seller capability, account demand, access, funding or procurement approval established
