# UK Financial Services — SIC K, wider than Banking

**Candidate research, 14 November 2026; deep tier partial.** The national financial system includes households, nonfinancial firms and the state. SIC K measures production by financial/insurance activities. Banking is a narrower lending/deposit/intermediation subset. Neither national debt nor banks' balance sheets measure K's sales market.

The common ONS basis puts K at **81/1000 of 2024 GVA**, with **September 2026 real GVA +1.1% versus September 2025**. This is neither 2026 nominal revenue nor a Banking share. Sources: [ONS GDP GVA3](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpmonthlyestimateuk/september2026/pdf), [SIC classification](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/classificationsandstandards/ukstandardindustrialclassificationofeconomicactivities). See source IDs ONS-GDP-SEP26 / ONS-SIC07.

## Competitive and external analysis

**Five Forces — bounded interpretation.** Rivalry is product-specific: deposit pricing differs from insurance risk selection, fund performance/distribution and market-making. Entry barriers differ between bank capital/liquidity/licensing, insurer solvency/claims capital, and advisory/software services. Substitutes include direct market funding, in-house treasury and passive investment, but volumes and effects require segment data. Supplier power can arise in cloud, market data, clearing or distribution; actual concentration, exit rights and spend are unknown. Buyer power differs between corporate mandates, retail deposits and institutional fund allocations. No numeric force score or cross-activity market-power ranking is justified.

**PESTLE.** Political: HMT's 14 July financial-services strategy update is a policy implementation statement, not delivered enterprise gain (HMT-FSG26). Economic: November Bank Rate, September household/deposit/mortgage rates and earlier credit survey alter possible pricing/financing mechanisms, with mismatched periods retained (BOE-MPC-NOV26 / BOE-MC-SEP26 / BOE-CCS-Q326). Social: national household income improved; current segment access, vulnerability, complaints and insurer policyholder outcomes remain missing (ONS-QSA-Q226). Technological: FPC highlights AI/cyber interconnectedness, while Lloyds already has announced AI capabilities; neither proves an unfilled sector need (BOE-FPC-SEP26 / LBG-ENVOY26). Legal: bank, insurer, investment and payment permissions differ; exact activity/entity/effective rule is required, not a general compliance label. Environmental: the CCC national transition assessment supplies context; financed-emissions/claims exposure and enterprise response remain unverified (CCC-PROGRESS26).

**Relational SWOT.** Strength context: official banking resilience and existing enterprise capabilities counter a universal fragility thesis. Weakness: current intermediation/service shortcomings outside banks are insufficiently acquired, not asserted. Opportunity hypothesis: improve a specific customer funding/risk/service outcome after current internal/incumbent solutions are understood. Threat hypothesis: market/liquidity, operational and repricing transmission can matter, but must be tied to a portfolio/activity before enterprise use. No shared macro risk becomes an automatically qualified Material Pressure.

## Useful decision question and missing deep work

For an insurer, manager or payment intermediary: **which actual client outcome or operating decision changes after the current financing/risk/service arrangements, and is any remedy still open?** Role categories: product/underwriting/investment, operations, risk and finance; actual authority unknown. First acquire a named current firm's matched-period results, permitted activity, capital/claims/flow measures, existing programmes and outcome evidence. An empty enterprise agenda is preferable to extrapolating Banking conclusions across K.
