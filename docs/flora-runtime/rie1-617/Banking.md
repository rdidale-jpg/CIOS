## Funding, capital, customers and operating value chain

Functional chain: attract deposits/wholesale capital → treasury/liquidity and credit allocation → origination/underwriting → servicing/payments → collections/recovery/customer outcomes. Funding price, asset yield and credit quality interact with operations, capital and conduct costs. A deposit is a liability stock, not revenue; lending is not profit; capital ratio is not an available project budget.

BoE's September effective rates differ for new mortgages (4.63%), existing mortgage stock (4.03%), new time deposits (4.42%) and existing sight deposits (1.66%). Possible repricing/retention mechanisms require individual product, maturity, hedge and customer behaviour evidence (BOE-MC-SEP26). The earlier credit survey reports lower SME availability **and demand**, so weaker lending cannot be attributed solely to bank refusal (BOE-CCS-Q326). November's Bank Rate change is later than these September observations (BOE-MPC-NOV26).

Lloyds September CET1 is 13.3%; NatWest 13.5%; these are not risk-adjusted performance rankings or excess spend capacity. The FSR major-bank peer aggregate of 14.2% is **Q1**, not the September group ratios (BOE-FSR-JUL26 Table 6.A). FPC reports strong bank liquidity/capital, a material counter-case to universal funding distress (BOE-FPC-SEP26).

BoE operates RTGS and CHAPS (BOE-SETTLE). Membership/participant, service-provider and regulatory relationships require distinct evidence. Current bank-specific payment dependencies, customer-service complaints, cost-to-serve, outage performance and remedy evidence remain open.

## Five Forces — contrasting mechanisms, no arbitrary scores

1. **Rivalry:** retail deposit/mortgage repricing competes on rate and service; commercial banking adds relationship, credit capacity and transaction services. Different Barclays business mix prevents reading Group profit as retail competitive superiority. Current matched market shares/pricing response are gaps.
2. **Entry:** prudential capital/liquidity, authorisation, payment access, funding trust and operating resilience shape deposit-taking entry; specialist nonbank lenders do not replicate every bank function. No current entrant cost curve acquired.
3. **Substitution:** bond/private-credit/equity funding can substitute corporate loans, subject to access/terms. Savings/investment products differ from insured transaction deposits; no measured displacement asserted.
4. **Supplier power:** existing cloud/workplace/data/payment arrangements can constrain sourcing. Lloyds Microsoft and Google disclosures prove named relationships, not exclusivity, concentration percentage, exit failure or available displacement work.
5. **Buyer power:** a corporate treasury mandate differs from fragmented household choices and vulnerable customers. Observed deposit moves require product/customer mix and pricing evidence before inferring churn, dissatisfaction or bargaining power.

## PESTLE and strategic synthesis

Political: financial-services strategy is a policy plan, not approved buyer scope. Economic: rates, funding mix and impairment interact with balance-sheet hedges and customer affordability. Social: household aggregate improvement can conceal vulnerable cohorts; no named customer pain follows. Technological: Lloyds platform/AI programmes establish incumbent/internal alternatives. Legal: prudential, conduct, resilience and ring-fencing constraints apply by legal entity/activity; current enterprise-specific compliance findings are not acquired. Environmental: loan-collateral/portfolio transition may transmit climate exposure, but portfolio materiality and funded responses must be established before a bank-specific thesis.

**SWOT interpretation:** strong capital/liquidity and reported profitability are resilience strengths; no universal bank weakness is established. Repricing and reliable customer service are possible decision areas, subject to actual unmet outcomes. Systemic/operational exposure is a threat mechanism, not proof of inadequate controls. Existing platform investments and hedges can defeat an external pitch.

Two candidate strategic questions survive: (1) **where do repricing and service choices materially affect a defined customer cohort after hedges and retention actions?** Treasury/product/customer operations; validate actual cohort outcomes and available choice. (2) **which critical-service dependency still requires a decision after funded controls and incumbent contracts?** Operations/technology/risk; obtain permitted service mapping and test evidence. No customer-confirmed need, open procurement or opportunity value is established. See [agenda records](Agenda-Candidates.json).

**Deep standard partial:** matched sector profit pools/returns, bank-specific liquidity/funding mix, concentration, current law applicability, service outcomes, complete peer comparisons and supplier contracts remain material gaps. The next tranche must improve those dimensions rather than add more generic frameworks.
