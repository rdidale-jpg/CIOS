## 1. UK — turn investment into measurable productivity

**Proposed default insight:** “Investment is growing. Which operating changes are delivering useful productivity?”

RIE1 UK-A1 uses UK-PROD-RTI and UK-INV-Q3, sourced to ONS-PROD-Q226, ONS-GDP-Q326 and UK-STRATEGY26. Q2 preferred RTI-based output/hour grew 0.7% year-on-year; the LFS estimate was −0.2%. Q3 business investment grew 0.4% quarter-on-quarter. These different periods/methods do not establish a firm-level productivity deficit. The eight policy growth sectors are not the twenty SIC2007 sectors; comparable national scale is GVA, not sales TAM.

Relevant roles are industrial-policy, enterprise finance and operations, with no named buying authority. Government's existing industrial strategy and firms' existing capex/adoption programmes are management responses, not proof of additional purchasing. Strategy publication is a made decision; a specific asset/process/adoption allocation that remains changeable is **unknown**. Rising investment/output, method disagreement and satisfactory internal delivery are counter-evidence to generic distress.

**Next investigation:** select one named sector and authorised enterprise; reconcile output, hours, capex timing and adoption by process, then identify an outcome owner and a remaining decision. Do not derive private customer pain from UK aggregates. PESTLE supplies economic/policy/skills context; industry economics tests investment/output transmission; value chain and operating model locate adoption; Five Forces tests whether competitive economics actually make it urgent. Relational SWOT asks whether existing capability offsets the external pressure.

**Commercial improvement:** replaces a broad “UK productivity problem” sales story with a scoped test, a method countercase and a reason to hold back.

## 2. Banking — income growth and funding competition are different questions

**Proposed default insight:** “Which franchise can sustain returns as funding, product mix and credit costs change?”

RIE1 BANK-A1/A2 and RIE2 Banking-Comparability provide the analysis. Lloyds H1 Group underlying net income rose from £8,914m to £9,747m and underlying profit from £3,561m to £4,215m. NatWest Retail reports H1 income £3,438m versus £3,134m, hedge gains alongside mortgage-margin pressure and stable overall deposits with changing savings mix. Barclays UK has a different IFRS8 perimeter; L&G core operating profit is an APM for an insurance/asset-management business, not Banking. FPC's strong capital/liquidity assessment and Q1 cohort CET1 of14.2% counter a universal bank-crisis claim. Sources: R2-LBG-H126, R2-NW-H126, R2-BAR-H126, R2-LG-H126, BOE-FSR-JUL26 and BOE-FPC-SEP26.

Roles: finance, treasury, retail/product pricing and risk; precise budget/authority unknown. Existing hedges, deposit pricing, portfolio choices and credit models are made responses. Remaining product/service trade-offs are unverified. Income and profit growth, stable aggregate deposits, deliberate savings pricing and higher credit charges caused partly by model/comparator effects all constrain a “lost customers need a new platform” pitch.

**Next investigation:** obtain matched product/perimeter income, funding price/volume/mix, credit and capital bridges for one bank; establish whether a remaining service/pricing/operating choice has material incremental consequences. Do not rank incompatible cross-bank profit levels. Five Forces distinguishes deposit/customer bargaining, fintech/market substitutes and rivalry; banking economics ties spreads/fees/impairments/capital to returns; the value chain separates funding, origination and servicing. PESTLE rates/regulation transmit only through a verified entity/exposure. SWOT compares relative capabilities and threats without treating a peer's advantage as this bank's unmet demand.

**Commercial improvement:** supplies a defensible competitive investigation rather than a cross-bank league table or generic transformation pitch.

## 3. Lloyds — asset returns after depreciation

**Proposed default insight:** “Retail income is improving. Where do asset and service economics still leave a decision?”

LBG-A1 RIE2 uses R2-LBG-H126, R2-LBG-Q326 and R2-LBG-STRATEGY. Retail H1 underlying other income increased £130m while lease depreciation increased £129m: only £1m net movement **at that bridge**, not leasing-product profitability. Retail underlying operating costs fell £50m while Group operating costs rose £2m; cost movement includes lower severance and is not a pure productivity measure. Retail underlying profit improved £282m. Q3 extra used-car depreciation did not repeat. Observed charges are demonstrated accounting effects; an avoidable service/control failure or unmet need is **not** demonstrated.

Roles: CFO, COO and the relevant Consumer/Connected Finance asset/service owner, with exact responsibility and authority unverified. Existing lease risk mitigation, cost savings and strategic plans are made responses. The potentially open choice is a specific asset-risk, servicing or retention intervention beyond those remedies; its existence is unknown. Improving profit, non-repeating charges and satisfactory existing controls can falsify the thesis.

**Next investigation:** an authorised product/asset return bridge separating fleet mix, vehicle prices, depreciation, disposal gains/losses, servicing cost/outcomes and current controls; ask which decision can still change the net result. Do not attach contract value. Enterprise economics and value chain locate the asset/service result, operating model establishes actual ownership, Five Forces tests leasing/product rivalry and residual customer value, and relational SWOT tests whether current capabilities already address the exposure.

**Commercial improvement:** replaces “cost pressure” with a concrete, bounded first conversation and a credible no-intervention alternative.

## 4. Lloyds Bank PLC — customer outcomes without a causal shortcut

**Proposed default insight:** “What complaint root causes, if any, reveal avoidable service effort, and do current remedies address them?”

LBG-A2 RIE2 sources R2-FCA-H126/R2-FCA-METHOD, R2-FOS-APRSEP26 and existing capability disclosures. FCA legal-firm calendar H1 shows118,762 received/118,861 closed all-product complaints, 66.12% of closed complaints upheld by the firm, with55.25% closed within3days. That is complaint-handling workload, not unique customers, complaint cause, financial loss or a service-failure trend. FOS3,764 new cases cover **April–September**, not the same cohort/window. The Consumer Credit table is separate. Ipsos scores/ranks remain unadmitted because provider labels were not visually aligned. Only received-total is in the narrow A slice; other measures remain B.

Roles: Consumer/customer operations, conduct/compliance and finance; Group role does not establish legal-firm authority. Existing customer/digital investments and complaint closure processes are responses already made; their effectiveness requires journey/cohort data. The potentially open decision is a cause-specific improvement beyond current remedies, not a replacement-platform procurement. Company capabilities, prompt closures, denominator limitations and the absent matched trend counter an inference that every customer is dissatisfied or that AI caused harm.

**Next investigation:** permitted aggregate root causes, repeat contacts, eligible journeys, outcome cohorts and cost-to-serve, reconciled to legal entity and current remedies. No personal complaint files, FOS/FCA escalation ratio, fabricated harm or per-case cost. Operating-model/service value chain connects causes to journeys; PESTLE conduct context informs applicability; relational SWOT tests current controls/capability against actual outcomes; competitive positioning awaits correctly labelled comparable survey evidence.

**Commercial improvement:** moves from “complaints imply pain” to a specific, commercially relevant diagnostic with privacy and attribution limits.

## 5. Lloyds — net AI value within existing capabilities

**Proposed default insight:** “Where does AI improve a complete customer journey after controls, rework and operating cost?”

LBG-A3 RIE2 uses R2-LBG-AI26, R2-LBG-MS26, R2-LBG-ENVOY-PDF and R2-LBG-H126. Around £50m2025 GenAI value is company-reported; more than £100m additional2026 benefit is expected, not independently verified net outturn or supplier budget.40,000 Copilot licences/97% active among licensed colleagues and10,000+ engineers establish reported adoption, not net value or customer benefit. Microsoft's multi-year agreement and Envoy on Google Cloud are important existing commitments. Contract spend, end dates, legal counterparties, workshare, exclusivity, open scope and net benefits remain unknown.

Roles: COO, finance benefits owner, relevant journey owner and technology/control roles, not inferred named buyers. Platform/vendor commitments and designed oversight are made decisions. A residual workflow, benefits-attribution or hand-off decision may remain open, but must be established. Existing platform/control capability, internal teams, successful delivery and measurement-only remedies counter a missing-agent-platform pitch. Complaint totals cannot demonstrate these technologies failed.

**Next investigation:** one authorised eligible journey's baseline, adoption, quality, exception/rework, human controls, unit operating cost, existing contractual responsibilities and incremental net outcome. Distinguish actual benefit, expected benefit, costs and attribution; ask whether any choice remains influenceable before suggesting an offer. Enterprise economics evaluates net value; operating model/value chain identify ownership and hand-offs; Five Forces/customer value tests whether improvement affects competitive outcomes; SWOT explicitly pairs existing capability with external pressure.

**Commercial improvement:** turns a headline benefits claim into a practical net-value investigation, with an incumbent-aware entry play and no invented £100m opportunity.
