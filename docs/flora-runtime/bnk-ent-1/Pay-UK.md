# Pay.UK · operating a shared payments system

**Assessment, 18 November 2026.** A payment operator is an enterprise; a scheme, switching service, standard and settlement bank are different objects. Financial and source-use gaps remain explicit.

## Executive judgement

Pay.UK creates value by coordinating dependable common payment services, rules, access and change across institutions with different interests. Its mechanism is neither deposit-funded lending nor hyperscaler capacity sales. Transaction **value** passing through a system is not operator revenue or supplier addressable spend. Cost recovery, reliability, governance and coordinated change are the useful analytical frame.

The commercially useful inquiry concerns a specific unresolved service/change decision and its participant consequences. No unfunded programme, open tender, failed control or available external budget is established. Incumbent arrangements counter an assumed greenfield opportunity.

## Economics and financial depth

The **RCMS** FAQ describes cost recovery, 2025–26 billing through direct FPS participants, and a move toward direct use/behaviour billing expected by 2027. It also describes the organisation as not-for-profit. This is a **service-specific disclosure**, not a complete current company tariff or proof of a delivered 2027 change. No price is copied or applied to a bank. [BE-PAY-ECONOMICS]

**Interpretation:** common infrastructure and assurance incur fixed/variable service costs. Funding decisions must sustain continuity and change while benefits may extend beyond directly paying participants. A not-for-profit model still needs financial resilience and resources; it is not evaluated by bank NIM or shareholder-profit maximisation.

Cash, supplier commitments, working capital and resilience resources differ from bank prudential capital or cloud infrastructure investment. No turnover, surplus, reserve, allocation or funding deficit is asserted: the official **2025 annual-report PDF exceeded the retrieval tool's size limit**, so financial pages were not inspected. The homepage's rendered counter lacks a safely established unit/denominator and is omitted. Financial coverage is therefore less mature than the two bank assessments.

**Processes:** maintain rules/access → coordinate operation → monitor exceptions → manage continuity and controlled change. The published COO and risk/finance/strategy roles provide context; service-specific delegated accountability remains unverified.

## Commercial investigation — Owner/research only

**Relational SWOT:** an established common-service operator, guarantor governance and incumbent infrastructure are strengths. Participant incentives and common funding may complicate change, but a deficit or failed programme is unproved. Specific remaining continuity/access/information decisions may be investigable only after validation. Adequate funding, existing commitments and effective governance are counter-evidence.

**Specific questions:** Which decisions remain open after incumbent extensions? What is each service's funding/accounting perimeter? Which benefits accrue to paying participants versus wider users? Who owns operator versus participant controls? What demonstrates delivered outcomes rather than a programme label?

**Falsifier:** fully scoped commitments, adequate funding and satisfactory outcomes with no remaining decision gap remove the commercial thesis. Readable accounts, current service-use permissions, exact supplier legal parties and delivered-change evidence are prioritised closure work. No buyer need, contract value or opportunity is manufactured.
