# Passenger rail and FirstGroup — who carries revenue risk?

Research cut: **18 November 2026**. Research-only; no accepted Model or public source grant.

Entity references: `FIRST GBR DFTO GB-RAIL-FREIGHT`. Claim trail: `GBR-1 FST-1 FST-2 FST-3 IN-FIRST FR-1`.

FirstGroup supplies a useful differentiated operator example because contracted rail, open access, commercial bus and franchising do not share the same exposure. Its adjusted turnover deliberately removes gross DfT flows where it takes substantially no revenue risk, while retaining management/performance fees. A simple turnover ranking would obscure the business. The measurement ledger preserves both reported bases rather than synthesising a common margin.

Proposed value streams are planning a service, staffing and preparing vehicles, carrying passengers safely, managing disruption and earning fares or contract fees. Proposed capabilities include driver competence, depot readiness, timetable execution, ticket distribution, fleet maintenance and concession compliance. Open access adds route-demand, yield and capacity decisions; an authority concession adds contractual performance and mobilisation discipline. They are neither generic IT estates nor transformation programmes.

Portfolio changes need dated treatment. FirstGroup's H1 review records the London Overground start and the prior SWR transfer. Mistral Data is recorded as sold, not a current internally owned technology business. Graham Sutherland is named CEO in the 17 November issuer results; no subsidiary-level buying authority is inferred. Recruitment/training and launch/ramp expenditure are existing responses to operating execution, not evidence of neglected management.

GBR is kept as a proposed institutional counterpart. DFTO is an existing separate legal company; the official page and register do not prove every current operator holding. The licence consultation cannot authorise a premature Network Rail/TOC identity merger. Devolved Scottish ownership remains separately sourced.

Priority next research is current GWR/Avanti transfer instruments and retained fee/obligation treatment, current concession legal parties, operator-level customer outcomes and maintenance/rostering interfaces. A useful commercial discriminator is whether the issue sits with revenue optimisation, a contracted reliability obligation or transition mobilisation. Existing operator programmes, staff training and in-house data capabilities are counter-evidence to a generic transformation pitch. No future transfer date or current tender is invented.

## Freight is a separate operating model

ORR’s April–June2026 GB release reports freight volumes and cancellations separately (FR-1). Train-km, tonnes and net tonne-km describe different utilisation/workload dimensions; none prices a haulage contract or proves a modal shift. Proposed capabilities include consist/load planning, path and terminal coordination, traction/crew availability and wagon utilisation. Passenger fares and public-service fees cannot substitute for freight revenue. Commodity mix, specific operator economics, customer agreements and port-to-rail handoffs remain next-tranche demands.
