A UK Industry Twin must not generalise England funding or GB infrastructure to every nation. The Scottish sponsor explicitly separates government, Scottish Rail Holdings and its passenger subsidiaries. Northern Ireland's NITHC/Translink portfolio includes bus and rail under a different governance/source route; its corporate index is not a verified financial dossier. Wales, local authorities, trust ports and municipal transport retain named research demands rather than being represented by TfL.

These actors need their own economic grammars. A sponsor allocates funds and defines outcomes; a holding body oversees companies; a regulator sets or monitors obligations; an operator delivers service. None is simply a 'transport customer' with commercial turnover. NITHC's strategy title is not an implemented operating-model transformation or proof of measurable benefits.

GBR remains proposed in the November licence consultation. Its described future responsibilities must not overwrite Network Rail, DFTO or devolved ownership records. DFTO's registered identity and official government page are a starting point; current exact subsidiary transfer records are still required. Government expenditure, subsidy received by operators and access payments are connected flows and cannot all be added into industry scale.

Proposed institutional capabilities are outcome commissioning, funding allocation, safety/economic oversight, asset stewardship, contract management and passenger accountability. These are analytical role decompositions. Named current officials and detailed statutory powers require current official instruments before assignment.

Next work should verify Wales transport bodies and devolved highways/ferry arrangements, current NITHC accounts and operating subsidiaries, and the evolving rail legislative timetable. Source technical limits and institutional complexity are not evidence exhaustion. No relationship to a bank or utility is invented to fill a cross-sector diagram. Existing governance and commercial boundaries remain separate, preserving the option to compare roles without constructing a second canonical ownership model.
