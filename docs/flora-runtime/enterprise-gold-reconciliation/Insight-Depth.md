| Service activity | Lloyds Bank PLC, Jan–Jun 2026: 115,687 banking/card complaints received; 115,798 closed; firm-upheld 66.37%. Existing FCA product profile and totals | Product scale, case mix or repeat contact are hypotheses; the return does not discriminate them | Actual case activity and firm decisions. Neither labour hours, redress, harm nor financial loss follows from the counts | The return does not identify a complaints-specific remedy or effectiveness. Closed cases are not proof of improved outcomes | Customer denominator, repeat contacts, complexity, handling effort, remedial results and service-owner mandate | For the selected product, do case types recur after a firm-upheld decision, and what outcome follows the current remedy? |
| Aggregate outcome | All-product firm-upheld 66.12% across 118,861 closed cases. Calculated banking/card share of closed cases is 97.4% | The aggregate is strongly shaped by the closed-case product mix; product cohorts differ | The overall rate does not describe identical outcomes across products. It is not a customer harm or quality ranking | No intervention is evidenced by aggregation itself | No comparable prior-period series or matching product/customer denominators in the eligible slice | Establish cohort, product and customer comparability before drawing a performance conclusion; avoid a volume-only CEO issue |
| AI value | Management-reported FY2025 Group contribution of about £50m; later management expectation above £100m for 2026 | Platform/data capability can enable delivery, but the disclosures do not attribute contribution to Envoy, Copilot or a division | Reported contribution and a later expectation are distinct. No delivered-growth rate, progress percentage or full-cost net return is established | Envoy controls are a disclosed existing capability. The source does not prove effectiveness or that it caused the Group contribution | Workflow attribution, full costs, realised 2026 result, independent outcome evidence and open decisions | Use the existing distinct goal question: which realised benefit survives model, infrastructure, change and control costs, and who signs off attribution? |
| People and organisational change | Copilot licence disclosure and brand-transition statement excluding related role reductions | Product deployment can support work; neither licence count nor one programme's role statement explains Group workforce change | No employee/FTE count, active adoption, capacity saving or sourcing decision can be derived | Narrow disclosed brand transition; not an organisation-wide employment or outsourcing programme | Current workforce totals/splits, productivity, ownership and actual wider staffing change | Start from a named workflow and verified people outcome, and inspect the existing programme rather than proposing generic workforce reduction |
| Funding and efficiency | Group deposits 496.5bn at Dec 2025 and 502.8bn at Sep 2026, with existing calculated stock difference; dated future efficiency/savings ambitions | Mix, repricing, consolidation and transfers may affect balances; cost ratio can change with income as well as expense | Arithmetic stock change only; not organic inflow, cheaper funding, margin improvement or current cost performance | Disclosed platform/retirement and investment priorities; future goals are not achieved benefits | New segment/P&L/capital bridges remain research candidates under existing Source/use constraints; no capability allocation | Match deposit mix/repricing and income/expense/remediation basis before diagnosing an economic problem or proposing external scope |

No finding above is promoted to a durable Material Pressure. The large complaint share alone does not pass those gates. No Opportunity or Pipeline action is created.
