## Evidence and economics

Central-bank settlement supports stability and finality, not a retail spread/profit model. Bank-held settlement balances differ from commercial-bank customer deposits and payment throughput.

Exact factual propositions: BOE-1, BOE-2, BOE-3, I-BOE. See [claim ledger](../Claims.json) for source/period/caveat; figures are not recopied into a second financial model.

## Operating model

Conceptual enterprise value chain: Maintain eligible settlement accounts → Receive/validate obligation → Provide liquidity/control checks → Settle irrevocably → Reconcile and maintain service continuity. This is not a verified organisation chart or complete stakeholder value stream. A subsequent Inside handoff must establish the customer/participant, trigger, fulfilment hand-offs and delivered outcome.

- **Settlement finality** — Discharge obligations in central-bank money. Required information: Rules, participant account and irrevocability. Conceptual capability; actual system/team owner unverified.
- **Intraday liquidity management** — Enable settlement without inventing commercial lending. Required information: Collateral/liquidity arrangements and operational use. Conceptual capability; actual system/team owner unverified.
- **Service resilience** — Recover/operate the end-to-end payment service. Required information: System incident, tested recovery and participant obligations. Conceptual capability; actual system/team owner unverified.

RTGS/CHAPS Board belongs to wider Bank governance; operator and supervisory responsibilities differ. PRA remains within the Bank; a second independent corporate hierarchy would misrepresent ownership.

RTGS, CHAPS and messaging interfaces are evidenced at system scope. A listed direct participant does not prove all its other scheme roles or outsourcing vendors.
