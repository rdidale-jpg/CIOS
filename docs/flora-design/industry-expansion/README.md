Electricity is the worked example for this sector. Gas, water and retail economics need separate treatments. The BT/ServiceNow example lets the reader distinguish integration or delivery questions from a named product relationship, and prior consolidation from rollout and pilot. ## Interaction walkthrough

1. Choose **Telecommunications**. Select **Provide access**, then **Enterprise Economics**. The landscape stays in place while the lower panel shows the split between wholesale access and retail bills. In **Facts**, read BT’s supplied profile. In **Insight**, see the question about business-unit ownership.
2. Open **Ecosystem** and **Evidence & uncertainty**. Inspect the TEL-001 reference, candidate scope and unknowns. Escape or Close returns focus to the evidence button. Under **Enterprises**, open BT Group or Openreach for the candidate record in context.
3. Choose **Energy & Utilities** and open **Change over time**. Read the undated regulatory sequence, then open **National Grid** to see the missing evidence. The Facts example describes what the draft playbook says. Insight asks which obligation applies.
4. Choose **Technology** and open **Ecosystem**. Inspect the BT/ServiceNow announcement, its June 2024 publication date, and the unresolved present-day scope. Go to **Change over time** to see prior consolidation, wider rollout and the pilot separately. Switch to Insight to see which follow-up question that distinction supports.
5. On portrait iPad, open the compact **Explore** disclosure to access the Left rail. Read details below the landscape. Browser Back restores the preceding view and reading mode.

## Critique

- Telecom’s physical network is immediate, but the wholesale branch can be read as a BT-specific revenue map. Retain the conceptual label beside the diagram and keep the candidate profile visibly separate.
- Energy’s visual cycle suits regulated networks. The sector title is broader than the worked example, so keep the electricity label visible and test whether it prevents readers generalising the cycle to retail or water. The preview has the weakest evidence coverage and leaves the enterprise jump at a research gap.
- Technology preserves its original artwork. Three opening roles make orientation quick, but compress intermediaries and overlapping roles. Test whether a seller can distinguish the original research population from the selected example. The ServiceNow link opens the retained research file rather than a verified runtime enterprise route.
- The large landscape consumes more vertical space in portrait. Compact Explore keeps it prominent, while source inspection and economics may require scrolling. Verify on hardware with touch, keyboard and VoiceOver before choosing production breakpoints.
- The diagrams convey sequence and relationships through placement, with no implied quantities. The first-glance comprehension objective still needs timed seller testing.

## Implementation recommendations after design acceptance

Reuse existing shell, reading mode and source inspection patterns. Keep the industry-specific illustrations and diagrams as presentation over existing read owners. Pilot the Telecom industry and its existing TEL-001 candidate links first. Confirm current view eligibility, identity and source permissions before replacing preview anchors with governed runtime destinations. For Technology, test the economics view against current visual-world navigation and leave research attribution and canonical identity states intact. Keep Energy conceptual until an eligible regulated-business record and the relevant dated decisions are available.

Carry source status, claim scope, publisher/event dates, review state and uncertainty from existing owners. Conceptual and dated/candidate marks must remain distinguishable by text and shape. Check withdrawal, restricted destinations, keyboard focus, browser history, text enlargement and both iPad orientations. Use an eight-second seller comprehension test followed by proof retrieval and an enterprise-selection task. This pack uses additive design files and a byte-identical review copy of existing Technology artwork.
