# FLORA — Discovery, Progress & Engagement Strategy

**Strategy and experience architecture paper · v0.1 · 13 November 2026**  
**Status:** Proposed, documentation-only. Not accepted architecture, not implemented, not a commercial commitment.  
**Owner:** FLORA / CIOS  
**Related:** [Experience Roles, Professional Tools & Monetisation — draft PR #589](https://github.com/rdidale-jpg/CIOS/pull/589); [Offer-to-Sales v2 Sprint A — PR #588](https://github.com/rdidale-jpg/CIOS/pull/588); [Offer Library issue #587](https://github.com/rdidale-jpg/CIOS/issues/587).  
**Delivery constraint:** Preserve ongoing Offer-to-Sales sprints and live FLORA. No runtime implementation, merge, production deployment, cost-incurring agent or automation is authorised by this paper.

## Executive proposition

FLORA's beautiful, illustrated economy, industry and enterprise intelligence makes it inviting to explore. To become a professional tool people **choose to return to**, it should help them make durable progress toward goals they genuinely value.

**The engagement doctrine: reward meaningful progress, not time spent.**

We propose an optional, role-aware experience system with six interoperable elements:

1. **Discovery Expeditions** — visually rich, guided journeys through an economy, industry, enterprise, challenge or change event.
2. **Personal Missions** — real user goals broken into evidence-grounded steps.
3. **My FLORA Progress** — persistent, private record of completed work, knowledge, decisions and next actions.
4. **Ask FLORA Challenges** — context-aware conversational guidance that helps users investigate, visualise and complete a meaningful task.
5. **Living Intelligence** — user-authorised awareness of material changes relevant to their saved topics or work.
6. **Achievements and Collaboration** — optional acknowledgement of genuine outcomes and permissioned team/classroom missions.

These are **not six new data repositories or autonomous agents**. They should be a thin experience and work-product layer atop governed industry, enterprise, research, source, Commercial Mission, Offers and Pipeline owners.

## 1. Why engagement matters commercially

FLORA's potential audiences have different return rhythms. Salespeople may act every working day; teachers plan weekly/termly; investors respond to earnings; architects revisit decisions at project gates; regional planners follow funding and statistical releases. The objective is **helpful re-engagement at the right moment**, not an artificially high daily active usage statistic.

Commercial hypotheses to test:
- guided first-use missions lower time-to-first-value and improve activation;
- saved, useful work products raise retention and switching costs by making future visits more valuable;
- material source-backed changes create trust and meaningful natural return triggers;
- experiences with recurring professional outcomes can support paid subscriptions, team seats, institutional agreements and specialist tools;
- free Discovery Expeditions can distribute FLORA's distinctive visual experience and introduce professional upgrades without creating an unbounded compute subsidy.

These effects are **not established FLORA results**. Do not publish assumed conversion rates, pricing, retention improvements or return-on-investment claims without a measured pilot.

## 2. Visual and interaction philosophy

The approved FLORA visual contract is non-negotiable: one compact top navigation, illustration-first country/industry/enterprise experiences, unobtrusive economic context, no repeated source-inspection buttons or page-length gamification widgets.

- **Default:** an elegant, uncluttered landscape of relevant intelligence. Progress should not be emblazoned across every industry and enterprise.
- **Opt-in entry:** an optional small “My Journey” / “Continue” affordance in the My FLORA account or header; contextual prompts from **Ask FLORA** when requested.
- **In-scene interpretation:** exploration paths, subtle highlights of areas visited, choice of next stop, and ephemeral overlays. Do not imply sector size or verified industry fact from decorative image geometry.
- **Return experience:** a tasteful “Since you last explored…” panel limited to verifiable, material change and the next useful step. Dismissible, no guilt or streak-loss framing.
- **Progress workspace:** available on demand; goals, current steps, results and saved outputs, with the original image always one click away.
- **Accessible equivalent:** keyboard, screen reader, reduced-motion, high contrast, mobile/iPad portrait/landscape, and a non-image journey list.
- **User autonomy:** hide/disable any progress mechanism, take an expert fast path, pause notifications, export/delete personal journey data.

The UX should resemble an intelligent guide through a beautiful world, **not a dashboard of badges**.

## 3. Six experience components

### 3.1 FLORA Discovery Expeditions

An **Expedition** is a versioned, curated or approved user-generated learning and investigative path containing:
- title, objective, target experience(s), target audiences and estimated cognitive scope;
- ordered/branching *waypoints* referencing real, authorised FLORA countries, industries, enterprises, visual regions, source questions or scenario work;
- choices and questions that deepen interpretation without replacing canonical evidence;
- helpful “show me why”, “challenge this”, “take me to the next relevant company” Ask FLORA interactions;
- a meaningful **end artifact**: source-backed briefing, selected targets, completed case study, investment thesis, technical decision note, impact scenario or research hypothesis;
- completion criteria based on validated task steps, not raw clicks or minutes spent;
- editorial review, provenance/version, accessibility and source rights.

**Example A — Discover the UK AI Economy (Research/Explorer):** economy panorama → AI and cloud industry subareas → leading technology participants → ecosystem and dependencies → relevant AI disruption points → final sourced sector briefing with uncertainties. Do not treat unlabeled visual scenery as enterprise evidence.

**Example B — Aviation Transformation Opportunity (Sales):** commercial aviation industry → easyJet enterprise → evidence-supported transformation signals → the seller's authorised Offer portfolio → distinct entry-play options → customer validation questions → *user-confirmed* next account action. A mission completion must not automatically insert a qualified opportunity into Pipeline.

**Example C — An Aviation Business Case (Educator):** airline value chain → company comparison → PESTLE → teacher-approved exercise → feedback/assessment → source-cited lesson pack. Preserve student privacy.

**Example D — Clean Energy Futures (Sustainability):** country/energy system → sector technology changes → sourced grid/energy baseline → compare bounded scenarios → user-owned assumptions and impact report. Do not replace company emissions with generic grid intensity.

Expeditions can be free acquisition experiences; advanced creation/collaboration may be premium, subject to cost and content rights.

### 3.2 FLORA Missions

A **Mission** is a user-owned or team-authorised objective, distinct from the existing technical/agent Mission and the existing **CommercialMission**. Use a naming distinction such as **Progress Goal** in runtime to avoid collision.

A Mission has: goal, source/purpose, owner, scope, work product, ordered milestones, evidence/validation requirement, optional due date, completion/abandonment, user controls and status history.

Examples:
- Sales: “Find and validate three potential enterprise targets for my approved AI-first transformation Offer.”
- Researcher: “Test the hypothesis that cloud cost optimisation is reshaping UK enterprise demand.”
- Investor: “Build and revisit a company investment thesis after earnings.”
- Technologist: “Compare three viable architecture options with documented trade-offs.”
- Educator: “Prepare and deliver a case study for next week's class.”
- Social value: “Establish an impact evidence baseline and define measurable targets.”

Missions should support **continue, pause, revise, complete, abandon**. Progress comes from genuine user-confirmed work; the ability to abandon a mission safely is essential. Paid/protected actions remain gated by independent entitlement and security authorization.

### 3.3 My FLORA Progress

A private, persistent summary that shows:
- current mission and next useful action;
- completed meaningful work products, revised hypotheses and decision history;
- optional explored regions/topics and declared confidence/learning mastery;
- timely, grounded changes to followed entities;
- optional artefact history, sharing settings, revision and portability.

Sample progress semantics: “3 of 5 steps completed” is valid only where steps have independently checkable completion rules. “60% smarter about banking” is not valid.

Progress must **not** write to canonical Intelligence, alter industry research status, create seller capabilities, invent buyer demand or count unqualified prospects as pipeline. Completion events are not verified business performance.

### 3.4 Ask FLORA Challenges

Ask FLORA is the universal, context-aware explanatory and orchestration interface proposed in PR #589. A **Challenge** is a bounded guided interaction built upon it; not an unconstrained autonomous agent.

Four modes:
- **Explain:** “Why is this economic change significant?”
- **Investigate:** “Show the evidence and strongest counterargument.”
- **Visualise:** “Overlay my permitted target accounts and compare them with sourced industry signals.”
- **Act/Save:** “Save a brief, a lesson or an entry-play draft,” after explicit confirmation.

Challenges are optional and adapted to the selected experience, current enterprise/industry/visual selection, authorisations and user's source cutoff. Generated output is labelled as FLORA interpretation, source fact or user assumption accordingly. Tool scopes and source access must be server-enforced; an LLM's text cannot grant authority.

Sample chain:
1. User asks “Help me understand AI-first business transformation in banking.”
2. FLORA offers a choice: five-minute overview, deeper evidence-led exploration, or seller-specific opportunity investigation.
3. A bounded sequence uses existing visuals and verifiable research, asks one meaningful question at a time.
4. User can save a summary or stop at any time.
5. No hidden research spend, automatic Pipeline entry or contact outreach.

### 3.5 Living Intelligence and Material Change

Allow users to follow specific industries, companies, topics, sources, programmes or authorised account records. A future read-only **Change Engine** can identify evidence-backed updates and present them in context.

Quality requirements:
- distinguish source publication time, effective/event date and the time FLORA last reviewed it;
- identify whether an item is *newly observed*, *newly verified* or merely *reclassified*;
- explain relevance to the user's declared purpose and actual objects being followed;
- show contradictions/supersession, not just positive news;
- suppress duplicates, stale news, unfounded urgency and high-volume low-value alerts;
- require explicit permission for external email/mobile/third-party notifications, with pause and digest controls;
- respect API quotas, licensing, privacy, model usage and per-tenant budget.

Do not claim monitoring exists until it is implemented and scheduled with actual resources. Initial version may use existing, locally refreshed dataset comparisons on page visit.

### 3.6 Achievements, Collaboration and Recognition

Suggested only where there is a legitimate basis for recognition:
- **Evidence Investigator:** evaluated competing sources and recorded an answer with uncertainty.
- **Case Study Complete:** finished a structured educational learning activity.
- **Decision Recorded:** compared alternatives and saved a defensible decision note.
- **Qualified Opportunity:** reached existing authorised Pipeline validation requirements; never just “created an opportunity card”.
- **Impact Measurement Ready:** documented a baseline, assumptions and follow-up measure.

Achievements should be private by default, dismissible and not gate access to important factual information. Avoid streaks, randomised rewards, scarcity tactics, public professional ranking or prestige claims without independent assurance.

Team challenges may be useful in *classrooms, sales teams and research groups*, but only with explicit team membership, permissioned work products, safe data visibility, and the ability to opt out. Never reward bulk sales opportunities, oversized revenue forecasts or unauthorised sharing.

## 4. Role-by-role engagement loops

| Experience | Signature Expedition or Mission | Natural return trigger | Verified/useful result | Bad proxy to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales / Business Development | Offer → account → trigger → entry-play journey | Meaningful target-account change, seller action or qualification milestone | Seller-reviewed next move, authorised pipeline progress | Number of leads generated |
| Account Director | Account health and whitespace mission | Contract, sponsor, service or portfolio change | Documented account growth plan | Number of accounts visited |
| Researcher / Analyst | Evidence-led industry investigation | New source, contradiction, research deadline | Versioned research report or thesis | Source-count leaderboard |
| Educator | Teach an industry/enterprise case | Teaching plan and class schedule | Lesson delivered and reviewed | Logins or question attempts |
| Student | Guided industry mastery journey | Genuine learning milestone/course timetable | Demonstrated understanding | Longest daily streak |
| Technologist / Architect | Technology decision expedition | Programme gate, dependency or vendor change | Recorded decision/trade-off | Number of architecture diagrams |
| Investor / Corporate Development | Living investment thesis mission | Earnings, disclosure or material thesis change | Source-checked thesis revision | Return forecast “accuracy” without audit |
| Founder / Product Leader | Customer problem/discovery expedition | Experiment result, competitor change | Tested market/product hypothesis | Number of startup ideas |
| Clean Energy / Sustainability | Transition options and impact journey | Policy, energy source update, reporting or project gate | Comparable bounded scenarios | Unsourced CO2 savings |
| Social Value / Inclusion | Impact evidence mission | Programme reporting, community/contract milestone | Credible metrics and assessment | Declared commitments counted as delivered |
| Policy / Regulation | Policy-change expedition | Consultation, effective rule, evaluation | Verified policy brief and alternatives | Advocacy “wins” |
| Job Seeker / Career | Company research and connection exploration | Interview, vacancy, profile/contact update | Interview dossier, user-chosen networking plan | Contact imports or automated messages |
| Regional Development | Regional growth and industrial cluster journey | Government statistics, funding/event cycle | Decision-ready regional briefing | Number of map regions opened |

**Cadence follows work:** daily when useful for Sales, weekly/termly for Educators, quarterly/event-led for Investors, milestone-based for Technologists. No forced recurring engagement.

## 5. What is genuinely game-like versus merely useful?

| Mechanic | Recommended use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Progress tracker | Authentic multi-step work toward a meaningful artifact | Arbitrary activity completion |
| Exploration map | Optional visited/learned areas and next meaningful destinations | Implying visual discovery coverage equals validated research coverage |
| Optional challenge | Clear bounded task with guidance and user agency | Endless prompt-chains to inflate session time |
| Achievement | Credible completion of defined work | Badges for clicks or source viewing |
| Levels | Learning difficulty or capability progression when verified | Withholding evidence or factual truth |
| Unlocks | New learning exercises or advanced tools by entitlement | Artificially hiding important basic knowledge |
| Team mission | Collaboration with permissioned outputs | Coercive rankings or disclosure of private performance |
| Alerts | Relevant source-backed changes within opted-in limits | FOMO, manufactured urgency or notification spam |
| Streaks | **Not recommended** | Shame for absence or breakage |
| Random rewards | **Not recommended** | Addictive reward loops |

The paper uses “gamification” only as a design vocabulary. **Engagement is achieved through usefulness, discovery, competence, agency and progress.**

## 6. Reusable capability architecture

Proposed logical flow (not a new canonical stack):

```text
User's My FLORA experience + thematic interests + authorised workspace
                         |
Beautiful shared visual Industry / Enterprise / Macro experience
                         |
 Optional Expedition / Mission / Ask FLORA entry
                         |
     Experience Context + source cutoff + authorisation checks
                         |
  Read-only Industry/Enterprise intelligence and permissions
                         |
    Bounded, cited explanation / visual overlay / challenge
                         |
  User-confirmed private Progress Goal / saved work product
                         |
 Existing owner for Pipeline | Research Note | Lesson | Thesis | Decision
                         |
 Optional, quota-bounded follow/watch/material-change summary
```

### Candidate work-product contracts

**ExpeditionSpec**: immutable `expedition_id`, version, curator, reviewed date, audience, outcomes, waypoint/branch graph, referenced canonical object IDs, source requirements, accessibility copy, legal/licence constraints and completion criteria.

**ProgressGoal** (not runtime Mission): `goal_id`, owner scope, experience, target outcome, active step, version, task status, last interaction, saved artifact reference, permission boundary, optional schedule, pause/abandon state.

**ProgressEvent**: privacy-scoped, minimal typed event (saved artifact, completed step, user correction), origin, timestamp, supporting authorised artifact ID and confidence/quality state. **Never a substitute for Pipeline, source verification or regulated impact measurements.**

**FollowSubscription**: authorised watched object references, alert channel, cadence, last reviewed source version, budget and revocation; no monitoring process implied without an implemented task runner.

**ExperienceAchievements**: derived from eligible completion events when permitted, opt-in/share setting and evidence rules. Avoid mutable public score tables.

### Ownership and compatibility

- Reuse the existing verified human session, tenant/workspace/enterprise permissions, protected journal, employer approval boundary and Pipeline transitions.
- Reuse CommercialMission for relevant Sales preferences, but do **not** redefine the canonical CIOS technical/agent “Mission”.
- Read existing Industry/Enterprise/Market Participant Twins and evidence through their owners. Expedition progress belongs to the **user experience layer**, not canonical enterprise truth.
- Reference the Experience Role/Theme/Entitlement distinctions proposed in [PR #589](https://github.com/rdidale-jpg/CIOS/pull/589). Switching roles changes suggested expeditions and presentation, not permissions or facts.
- Ask FLORA, My Data overlays, Connections, Watchlists and Notebooks are proposed shared capabilities, not independently duplicated storage systems.
- All user-provided/exported/connected material must respect privacy and rights; prohibit public leakage, prompt-injection tool elevation and unauthorised redistribution.

**Safety invariant:** authenticated identity + applicable entitlement + verified domain permission + current record-state/approval + explicit confirmation for any consequential action. Public learning/view-only activities never grant sales or employer rights.

## 7. Measurement and commercial experiments

**North-star candidate:** *Monthly active users completing or materially advancing a meaningful, role-relevant artifact*, measured per experience and opt-in usage. Do not aggregate incomparable outcomes without a clearly stated denominator.

| Stage | Good metric | Necessary checks |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Visit → chosen experience/first expedition | Organic vs paid acquisition and real traffic |
| Activation | Time to first saved useful work product | Human usability, source quality and drop-off |
| Engagement | Mission milestone completed when naturally due | Not raw pages/session minutes |
| Retention | Return and valuable progress by role-specific cadence | Compare cohorts, not arbitrary daily streaks |
| Discovery quality | Cited conclusions, successful source checks, user corrections | Hallucination and stale-source monitoring |
| Commercial | Paid conversion after credible premium need, account expansion | Entitlement clarity, no deceptive prompts |
| Unit economics | Cost per successfully completed task/returning user | AI compute, data licences, infrastructure, support |
| Trust | Zero cross-tenant leaks; timely revocation; satisfaction | Privacy controls, consent and security validation |

Pilot research: baseline task completion and repeat visits with no guided journey, randomise opt-in exposure where ethical/feasible, collect qualitative value feedback, compare task quality/time and incremental cost. Control for role/cadence and evidence freshness. Do not treat a conversion uplift as established before experiments.

## 8. Recommended roadmap (after current Offers work)

**Stage 0 — Concept study (no runtime):** two beautifully designed storyboards and a simple user journey test; one Sales opportunity journey and one Research/Education exploration. Interview representative users, validate meaningful milestones, avoid click-reward framing.

**Stage 1 — Minimal Progress Goal:** one persistent private goal and completion tracking based on *existing* authorised action results; one small “Continue journey” entry in My FLORA. No background monitoring, scoring, LLM autonomy, paid calls or public competition.

**Stage 2 — Two Discovery Expeditions:** 
- UK Technology or Banking → enterprise → sourced insight → research briefing;
- Aviation industry → easyJet → seller's authorised Offer → validation questions → seller-confirmed account action, with Pipeline still separately approved.
Mobile/iPad/desktop, keyboard and reduced motion required. Distinguish preview fixtures from real source data.

**Stage 3 — Ask FLORA guided challenge:** limited, context-aware explain/challenge/visualise; provide source lineage and cost controls. Deliberate Save creates only authorised user-level work product; route through domain owners.

**Stage 4 — Living Intelligence pilot:** read-only material-change comparison for explicit follow-list, initially on page visits rather than continuous agents. Add scheduled alerting only with consent, a real scheduler, provider rights and proven economics.

**Stage 5 — Collaboration and monetisation proof:** trial institutional lessons or sales-team shared mission, role-aware entitlements, premium saved artifacts/export or team features; measure willingness-to-pay and contribution margin.

No stage authorises deployment. Follow established GitHub PR, security validation, ownership, preview, consent and release gates independently.

## 9. Acceptance tests for future implementation

1. An industry illustration remains recognisable, full-sized and uncluttered without engagement widgets.
2. An opt-in user can start, pause, resume, revise and abandon one goal and return to the same source-aware waypoint.
3. A fresh user can skip guidance, explore normally and reach their authorised professional tools directly.
4. Progress cannot increase by repeated refresh or replayed action; event idempotency and version checking hold.
5. Sales progress cannot be represented as “qualified pipeline” until the existing Pipeline owner accepts its qualification step.
6. An Educator or public viewer cannot access another user's personal missions, seller Offers, contacts or financial assumptions through direct URL, HTML, cache or export.
7. Switching experience does not alter source evidence, canonical Intelligence, workspace membership or subscription entitlement.
8. Citations, historical source versions, competing evidence and uncertainty remain visible on demand even in animated/exploration mode.
9. Reduced motion, keyboard, screen readers and responsive iPad/mobile views provide functionally equivalent journeys.
10. Personal progress is private, revocable and exportable/deletable under the appropriate retention policy.
11. Bounded update notifications occur only with opt-in, validated materiality, provider rights and budget; no artificial engagement alerts.
12. Existing strict security suites and inherited baseline are unchanged; no protected-owner writes, agent privileges or deployment settings are modified.

## 10. Open product questions

1. **Naming:** “Discovery Expeditions”, “Journeys” and “Missions” as brand language versus the CIOS technical Mission concept. Proposed: public “Expeditions” and “My Progress”; runtime “ProgressGoal”.
2. **First-run:** offer a guided invitation only after the user sees the scene, or during role selection? Proposed: after visual reveal, unobtrusive and dismissible.
3. **Persistence:** ephemeral unsaved expedition vs explicit Save? Proposed: ephemeral browse; deliberate Save for a durable user project.
4. **Personal vs team progress:** default private; team shared projects require explicit permissions.
5. **Content authorship:** central curated journeys first; later permissioned user-generated and institution-created paths with editorial requirements.
6. **Rewards:** avoid numeric global XP, encourage user-relevant milestone accomplishment.
7. **Monitoring:** user-controlled when-worthy digests and natural cadence; avoid indefinite cost obligations before validating demand.
8. **Evidence access:** essential source transparency is never withheld behind engagement achievements or paid progress tiers where the claim is otherwise viewable.
9. **Commercial prioritisation:** choose which roles validate free acquisition, deep retention and paid workflows rather than trying to serve every persona in the first experiment.

## 11. Strategic conclusion

**FLORA should be a place where users build something valuable every time they return: understanding, decisions, professional expertise, working hypotheses, learning resources, real commercial opportunities or measured impact.**

The attractive visual interface invites exploration. Expeditions give direction. Missions and Progress preserve momentum. Ask FLORA offers depth when asked. Living Intelligence creates timely relevance. Genuine user outcomes justify sustained use and eventual premium subscriptions.

The critical distinction is between **rewarding progress toward a valuable purpose** and **optimising compulsive engagement**. FLORA should pursue the former.
