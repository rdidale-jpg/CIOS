## 1. Executive summary

**FLORA helps strategic technology sellers identify where companies may need to change, connect those needs to what the seller can genuinely deliver, and develop evidence-backed opportunities before an RFP appears.**

The initial business is a focused UK **B2B subscription intelligence and opportunity development product**, targeting senior sellers and small strategic sales teams in technology services, digital transformation, AI-first business transformation, cyber, legacy modernisation and adjacent consulting propositions.

FLORA's enduring asset is not a purchased contacts database or a generic AI chat interface. Its product advantage is the combination of beautiful country/industry/enterprise understanding, **seller-specific Offers**, accountable company evidence, distinct buyer-trigger hypotheses, informed entry plays and an authorised, user-owned opportunity Pipeline.

**Recommended launch:** a deliberately bounded, founder-led UK design-partner programme followed by a paid private beta. **Do not launch mass-market, free education, unlicensed integrations or sophisticated AI agents first.** Those are credible future expansion options, not dependencies for a useful Sales product.

### Proposed launch sequence

1. **Secure foundation and quality:** complete Sprint B commercial/source review and Sprint C seller-controlled opportunity workflow; confirm stable deployment, recovery, tenant separation, policies and source rights.
2. **10–15 carefully selected design partners** recruited through warm professional introductions, with varied seller types and explicitly representative use cases.
3. **Evidence of value:** use FLORA to build a real, permissioned portfolio of Offers, investigate a specified target-account universe and validate opportunities or eliminate weak leads. Audit the outcome against existing sales practice.
4. **Paid private beta:** convert demonstrably useful use cases to a priced individual and small-team offering, with human-reviewed research and controlled feature/compute cost.
5. **Product-led expansion:** add recurring research/watchlists, CRM integrations and broader user roles only after the paid Sales use case and economics have survived real-world testing.

**North-star metric:** *Seller-confirmed, evidence-backed opportunities materially advanced through FLORA*, accompanied by source quality and revenue/cost metrics. Avoid counting unqualified hypotheses as actual deals.

### Executive recommendation

Invest first in **distribution, usability, trust and repeatable commercial value**. The technical platform is increasingly capable, but a green CI result is not evidence of willingness-to-pay, dependable source licensing or a production-ready multi-customer service.

## 3. Customer segmentation and beachhead

### 3.1 Launch ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Economic buyer:** Head of Sales, Strategic Sales Director, UK business development leader, sector growth leader, founder/MD at an established technology consultancy.

**Primary active users:** Strategic account executives, capture directors, enterprise BDMs, practice sales leads, pre-sales consultants.

**Employer:** UK technology consultancy, systems integrator, AI/automation adviser, managed cyber service provider or specialist enterprise-modernisation vendor; typically selling relatively high-value and/or consultative services with multi-month sales cycles, incomplete published pipelines and sophisticated buyer stakeholders.

**User's valuable job:** “I know what we can sell and where we are credible. Help me identify which actual enterprises merit proactive pursuit, exactly why, and how to start a commercially intelligent conversation.”

### 3.2 Initial segments ranked

| Segment | Buying problem | Why FLORA could win | Difficulty | Proposed priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boutique-to-mid-size UK technology consultancies / SIs | Limited strategic research capacity; pressure to grow qualified enterprise pipeline | Visual market understanding plus Offer→enterprise trigger and entry play | Moderate data coverage; limited admin capacity | **1** |
| Enterprise software, AI-first transformation and legacy-modernisation practices | Need to identify material operating-model and technology events before formal competition | Distinct differentiated offer playbooks and evidence-based change | Need strong technical/customer proof | **1** |
| Specialist cyber and managed services sales teams | Prioritise customers based on material residual need, not generic security fear | Control-aware trigger research; disconfirming evidence | Private/compliance needs and sensitive data | **2** |
| Large SIs and global consulting practices | Higher-value accounts and complex competitive selling | Potential strategic account workbench | Procurement, SSO, information security, long sales cycles | **2–3** |
| Individual consultants / strategic sellers | Personal account development and research | Fast time-to-first-value | Price sensitivity and switching cost | **1 pilot** |
| Educators / Researchers / Investors / Advocacy roles | Different outcomes, existing future tools/strategy | Shared intelligence leverage | Requires role-specific depth, economics and licences | **Later** |

### 3.3 Customer selection and conflicts

Founder-led introductions can materially accelerate access to senior UK technology sales practitioners. However, **FLORA must not use an employer's confidential pipelines, proprietary bid materials, company subscriptions, client data, work hours, IP or relationships outside permitted agreements**. Before commercialising, obtain appropriate independent legal/employment/IP/conflict review, particularly around current employment and overlapping accounts. Avoid assuming personal network access is a right to use organisational information.

No preselected company is a committed design partner until explicitly invited and consenting.

**FLORA is not currently best positioned as a direct substitute** for LinkedIn Sales Navigator (professional graph), Apollo/Cognism (contact intelligence), or HubSpot/Salesforce (CRM execution). ## 5. Value proposition and minimum sellable product (MSP)

### 5.1 First complete user journey

1. Join a verified workspace and, where authorised, select an employer.
2. Choose primary/secondary technology commercial categories and select specific Offers from the developed library.
3. Add seller-specific evidence, limitations and delivery rights; obtain employer review where required.
4. Select country/industry or target enterprises; investigate dated, sourced structural signals and possible events.
5. Compare several Offers for an enterprise, including counter-evidence, incumbent and unknowns; avoid false urgency.
6. Save a reviewed account shortlist and agree a customer-first entry play, an evidence gap and responsible next action.
7. Advance the opportunity only when the existing Pipeline owner accepts deliberate human qualification; capture what evidence supported the decision.
8. Return when relevant evidence changes or a user-owned selling milestone is due; scheduled automatic monitoring is **future**, not assumed.

### 5.2 The launch promise

**“Build a defensible target-account shortlist and a credible first-meeting hypothesis for your real Offers.”**

Suggested design-partner test: a seller connects their authorised portfolio and completes a five-account opportunity assessment in one facilitated session, comparing the outcome against their existing process. Target time and quality thresholds must be piloted, not invented.

### 5.3 Launch exclusions

No autonomous outreach, invented contact identities, LinkedIn scraping, proactive full-web research agents without controlled economics, automatic employer authority, automatic Pipeline writes, predictions of deal close date/value, individual stock-investment recommendations, or blanket claims of complete industry coverage.

## 6. Distribution, go-to-market and initial launch

### 6.1 Founder-led design-partner programme (proposed 8–12 weeks after readiness)

**Recruit:** 10–15 senior users across 6–10 organisations (subject to confidentiality and conflict review), balanced between boutique SIs, AI/digital transformation, cyber and modernisation providers. They should have **real offers**, a representative target-account list and willingness to provide candid feedback.

**Entry offer:** private product preview plus a structured strategic selling workshop. Limited concierge-assisted set-up acceptable, but track support time to avoid mistaking founder-delivered consulting for scalable software value.

**Demo narrative:** show the UK/industry landscape in under a minute; choose seller Offers; compare two companies with *different* buying logic; show dated source, counter-evidence, economic hypothesis and next question; save a reviewed shortlist. Avoid overloading the demo with Governance/Research admin screens.

**Distribution channels ranked:** trusted introductions → invitation-only peer demos → case studies using permissioned, anonymised outcomes → founder/executive thought leadership → targeted conferences/communities and partnership webinars → organic discovery through visual industry essays. Paid acquisition comes later, once conversion and retention economics are understood.

**Content strategy:** publish useful evidence-led UK sector briefings and “why this matters to transformation sellers” examples, link to core visual exploration, invite qualified pilot applications. Never republish copyrighted full filings or use named client success without approval.

### 6.2 Launch phases and exit gates

| Phase | Relative timing (conditional) | Gate to advance |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Technical/product hardening** | Immediately after current Sprint B/C work | Enterprise rights/identities, professional review, UX, tenant isolation and restore drills signed off |
| **B. Closed design partner pilot** | Next 8–12 weeks | ≥10 engaged practitioners; repeatable observed workflow completion; no material data leaks or unsupported buyer-intent claims |
| **C. Private paid beta** | Following pilot | At least 5 independent paying seats or 2 paid organisational pilots; auditable unit cost and payment/refund/privacy basics |
| **D. UK general availability (controlled)** | Only after stable paid beta | Clear terms/support/SLA limits, source rights, customer operations, onboarding, repeat usage and positive contribution prospects |
| **E. Scale and adjacent roles** | Later | Subscription economics and role demand justify dedicated research/educator/investor products |

These are **proposed targets**, not performance already achieved or guaranteed milestones. Reassess calendar dates once real implementation and legal decisions are known.

### 6.3 Sales funnel and operating process

- **Qualify design partner:** seller has a meaningful strategic Offer, real account-research need and authority to use submitted account information.
- **Discover:** show two authentic source-backed enterprise matches with one negative/counter-indicated case.
- **Activate:** create a private Offer portfolio and a seller-reviewed target shortlist.
- **Demonstrate value:** user confirms which recommendations changed their account priorities, led to a conversation or ruled out wasted effort.
- **Convert:** offer time-bounded paid subscription with clear resource limits.
- **Expand:** teams pay for governed collaboration, account research consistency, permissions and reporting only when delivered.

Track opportunity-introduction and actual sale outcomes with explicit user permission and no claim that FLORA caused an entire customer win without causal evidence.

## 8. Business plan — illustrative first full commercial year

### 8.1 Commercial model and definitions

**Revenue model:** subscription SaaS, annual commitments paid monthly or upfront as permitted. Cash collection, recognised revenue, ARR and bookings are separate measures. A contract sold in December does **not** deliver 12 months' revenue inside that calendar year.

**Scenario model below uses average billable paid seats throughout a full operating year**, rather than year-end seats, in GBP ex VAT. Values are internally constructed planning assumptions, **not a market forecast**. No customer agreements, pricing experiments, operational cost quotes or production licence negotiations are yet validated.

**Formula:**
- Annual subscription revenue = 12 × [(average Individual seats × £69) + (average Team seats × £129)].
- Contribution after variable delivery = revenue × (1 − variable cost ratio).
- Operating result before tax/finance = contribution − fixed annual operating expense.
- Fixed expense includes salaries/contractors, security/compliance, SaaS, support, marketing and administration; model founder remuneration separately if unpaid.

### 8.2 Three illustrative scenarios

| Year-1 operating assumption | Conservative | Base | Stretch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Average paid individual seats** | 50 | 200 | 500 |
| **Average paid team seats** | 10 | 50 | 150 |
| Subscription revenue | **£56,880** | **£243,000** | **£646,200** |
| Variable delivery/data/AI/support cost | 25% | 25% | 25% |
| Contribution after variable costs | £42,660 | £182,250 | £484,650 |
| Fixed operating costs (illustrative) | £85,000 | £175,000 | £350,000 |
| **Operating result before tax** | **−£42,340** | **+£7,250** | **+£134,650** |

**Interpretation:** The base case only just breaks even, and only **if** 200+50 seats are paying *on average* across the year and fixed costs stay within £175k. This does not establish a credible first-year forecast; it highlights sales and cost sensitivity. If the first commercial year ramps from zero, year-average seats are well below end-of-year seats and early operating losses/cash burn could be materially larger.

**Break-even illustration:** At the hypothetical £69/month individual price and 75% contribution margin, a £175k fixed annual cost base needs approximately **282 average individual-only paying seats** (175,000 ÷ [12 × 69 × 0.75], rounded up), excluding acquisition costs embedded outside the model. At the hypothetical £129 Team price, about **151 average team-only seats** on the same assumptions. Actual results depend heavily on mix, churn, source fees, model calls and staffing.

### 8.3 The expenses that matter

| Cost pool | Source of uncertainty | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Founder/operator and engineering | Architecture, onboarding, maintenance and support labour | Track hours, allocate realistic founder salary/opportunity cost; avoid assuming free founder time indefinitely |
| Infrastructure and resilience | Hosting, protected data, backups, monitoring, disaster recovery, tenant growth | Load test, restore test, capacity limits and regular ops budget |
| AI/research inference | Model calls, retrieval, source search, complex workflows and agent repetitions | No unbounded agents; quotas, model budget, caching, cost per useful output |
| Data licensing | Full financial/research/contact feeds and redistribution rights | Start official public sources, rights matrix and negotiated paid add-ons |
| Security, legal, compliance | Multi-tenant data, GDPR, policies, cyber controls, contractual risk | Privacy/legal review, ongoing tests, incident handling, insurance assessment |
| Sales and growth | Demo support, marketing, events, channels, customer success | Founder-led distribution first; conversion/cost attribution |
| Billing and collection | Merchant fees, refunds, VAT, bad debt and revenue accounting | Compliant payment vendor; conservative cashflow assumptions |

Official API pricing may change, and inference, tool and storage costs must be budgeted per workflow, not per token alone. Use https://platform.openai.com/pricing/ for current API baseline when selecting models.

### 8.4 Required financial planning before launch

Build a **monthly 24-month cash-flow and unit-economics model** after design-partner evidence, including:
- monthly new paid users, expansion, downsells, churn, renewal and annual-vs-monthly payment timing;
- per-seat/tier ARPU, discounts, conversion and cohort retention;
- customer acquisition cost (fully loaded), gross margin and contribution margin;
- AI/data cost per active individual, per firm and per completed account assessment;
- support hours per onboarding and per subscriber; team implementations;
- salaries/contractors, founder compensation and outside consulting;
- payment fees, tax/VAT and bad-debt provisions;
- opening cash, capital injections, monthly burn, runway and cash break-even;
- best/base/downside sensitivity and explicit decision triggers.

**Capital requirement cannot be responsibly stated yet** without realistic staffing, founders' income requirement, legal structure, year-one launch dates and supplier data contracts. Avoid treating an illustrative table as a funding recommendation.

## 9. Legal, IP, data rights and responsible commercialisation

**Hard preconditions:**
1. **Ownership/IP and employer conflict review:** confirm FLORA code/data/docs/content ownership, contributor rights, any work-product restrictions and confidentiality duties in current employment; obtain independent legal advice before commercial sales if warranted.
2. **Customer information and private data:** only customer-authorised imports, private enterprise records, user-approved external integrations. Minimise unnecessary personal information.
3. **UK GDPR and PECR:** conduct data-flow mapping, lawful-basis/legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate, privacy notice, retention/deletion, DSAR process, processor DPAs, international transfer assessment and relevant direct-marketing compliance. UK ICO guidance does not authorise indiscriminate B2B marketing or profiling.
4. **Third-party rights:** access to published annual reports and websites ≠ unrestricted full-text ingestion, redistribution, model training or commercial feed resale; build a source-by-source rights matrix.
5. **Marketing and product claims:** never say “verified buying intent” when only strategy/structural signal is observed; label economic scenarios with sources and bounds; avoid claiming accreditation or supplier rights.
6. **Security operations:** verify tenancy and all direct URLs, secrets management, production origin configuration, rate limits, backup/restore, audit, incident response, account recovery, admin access, dependency supply chain and an agreed vulnerability response.
7. **Commercial policies:** legitimate business entity, tax/VAT, terms, privacy/cookie notices, billing, cancellation/refunds, trial rules, information security summary and support channels. Check relevant UK and overseas rules.
8. **Children/education, finance and regulated advice:** future education and investor products may need additional permissions, data-source licences and specialist protections before launch.

Sources: UK ICO direct marketing https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/ and legitimate interests https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/ ; Companies House developer API https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/get-started .

**Special caution:** A public licensed API or an individual user's paid subscription does not necessarily confer the right to redistribute results inside a multi-customer commercial SaaS.

## 14. Strategic options beyond the beachhead

**Option 1: Cross-role research platform.** After Sales has traction, adapt governed research and visual intelligence for analysts and educators, with separate outcomes, quotas and institutional rights.

**Option 2: Integration-led professional hub.** LinkedIn export, Graph/HubSpot, market/financial and other authorised connectors can make FLORA more valuable, but data permissions, API approval and costs are prerequisites; do not market integrations before they work.

**Option 3: Ask FLORA / My Data / Missions.** A universal contextual interface, private overlays, guided expeditions and persistent work products could eventually make FLORA a multi-professional platform. They are deliberately not day-one launch scope.

**Option 4: Strategic enterprise intelligence licensing.** Enterprise/team contracts, API products and governed insights may be a higher-value model, but require robust rights, support, SSO/multi-tenant security and demonstrated procurement willingness.

**Option 5: Data-supported research services.** Carefully scoped human-led pilot research engagements could provide early insight and revenue, but distinguish consulting revenue from recurring product ARR and avoid excessive bespoke deliveries.

### Strategic discipline
Define explicit entry criteria for expanding beyond Sales: proven Sales activation; positive customer evidence; acceptable gross margin; unblocked source rights; a clear role-specific durable work product; low incremental cost to serve.

## Appendix A — Alternative social-purpose ownership structures (13 November 2026)

**Decision status:** Exploratory. The proposed for-profit SaaS framing in sections 1–15 is one option, **not the chosen constitutional structure**. This appendix compares commercially viable, community-interest and charitable alternatives. No legal incorporation, certification, subscription tax status, grant eligibility, charitable registration or ownership change is authorised.

### A1. Strategic reframe

FLORA could aim to democratise public economic, industry, enterprise and environmental understanding, while charging for valuable, private, professional applications such as seller-specific Opportunity Factory, team tools, investor analysis and premium research. The mission could be:

> **Improve access to reliable intelligence about industries, enterprises and economic change, enabling people to learn, make better decisions and contribute to social and environmental progress.**

This is **an aspirational product mission**, not a charity-law-approved charitable object. Free educational access alone does not make a company a charity; private benefit of corporate sales users and founders must be accounted for.

### A2. UK structural alternatives

### A3. Source-verified UK considerations

- **B Corp certification:** B Lab UK says it is for eligible for-profit businesses; charities and nonprofits are not eligible. UK limited companies must meet the stakeholder legal requirement, usually via amended articles. New V2 standards and third-party assurance apply; small/startup certification timing must be verified. Fees and standards can change. Official: https://bcorporation.uk/b-corp-certification/before-you-certify/who-can-certify/ ; https://bcorporation.uk/b-corp-certification/before-you-certify/legal-requirement/ ; https://bcorporation.uk/b-corp-certification/b-labs-new-standards-are-here/ ; https://bcorporation.uk/b-corp-certification/pricing/
- **CIC:** Company exists for community benefit. The statutory asset lock limits transfers and private gain; a CIC limited by shares can pay capped private dividends. Regulator guidance reports an aggregate dividend cap of 35% of profits; a CIC does not ordinarily convert back into an unrestricted Ltd. Official: https://www.gov.uk/set-up-a-social-enterprise ; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/community-interest-companies-how-to-form-a-cic/community-interest-companies-guidance-chapters
- **Charity/CIO:** Only charitable purposes for the public benefit; eligible purposes include appropriately framed education, but a commercial seller product is not automatically charitable. An England/Wales CIO is regulated by the Charity Commission, without Companies House registration. A charitable company limited by guarantee is another option. Official: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/public-benefit-rules-for-charities ; https://www.gov.uk/setting-up-charity/structures
- **Charitable trading:** HMRC distinguishes primary-purpose from non-primary-purpose commercial trading and describes the subsidiary option. The charity does **not** enjoy general VAT exemption just because it is a charity. Official: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charities-and-trading

These are high-level UK/England-Wales descriptions, not advice on eligibility, tax, governance or formation. Formal advice from a charity/corporate solicitor and accountant is required before incorporation or restructuring.

This is **a governance hypothesis, not a transfer plan**. ### A6. Mission metrics to test

- Number of truly free learners/educators with access to usable, independently sourced materials, adjusted for quality not login volume.
- Lessons completed, teaching materials deployed and demonstrable improvement in industry/economic literacy.
- Geographic/socioeconomic access and accessibility, measured with consent and privacy.
- Share of reliable public data and research that users can actually inspect under appropriate source rights.
- Low-cost tools used to support workforce upskilling, social value or environmental transition *without claiming unsupported causal effects*.
- Transparent annual public-impact report, source corrections and privacy/security incidents.
- Commercial side: seller-reviewed real opportunities, subscriber retention, gross margin, and **cost to fund public services** without an unbounded AI subsidy.

### A7. Recommended decision sequence

**Step 1 — Decide whether public benefit is a core legal purpose or an enduring commercial commitment.** If owner equity and broad platform innovation remain central, a mission-led Ltd is simpler. If the platform is primarily dedicated to public education/research and private benefit is incidental, explore charity/CIO seriously.

**Step 2 — Do not lock FLORA into a hard-to-reverse structure before testing willingness-to-pay, founder ownership/IP and source licences.** CIC asset-lock and charitable restrictions are material, long-term choices.

**Step 3 — Define a short impact charter now**, regardless of legal form: sourced public education commitment; a minimum free-use guarantee subject to transparent limits; user privacy; no fabricated buying intent; accessible learning; measurable impact with annual review.

**Step 4 — Seek qualified UK legal/tax advice** on Ltd/B Corp stakeholder articles, CIC limited by shares/guarantee, charity viability, trading subsidiary, grant restrictions, founder remuneration and IP/brand arrangements.

**Step 5 — Decide on a future legal structure before formal public fundraising, equity offering or commercial subscription launch.** Avoid prematurely representing FLORA as B Corp-certified, a registered charity or a CIC.

### A8. Current provisional recommendation

**Best reversible route:** establish the public-benefit mission and independent research/education commitments, while keeping FLORA a commercial Ltd (eventually pursuing B Corp certification if standards, operation duration and impact evidence support it). Preserve an option for a distinct charity or educational foundation once genuine sustainable cash flow and separately charitable public-benefit activity justify the overhead.

**Best strongly mission-locked option:** a CIC with carefully designed ownership and community-interest articles, if limiting private extraction is an intentional owner decision.

**Best charitable option:** a genuine independent education/economic-literacy charity, possibly with a commercial trading subsidiary, **only if** public benefit becomes the dominant objective and control/equity constraints are accepted.
